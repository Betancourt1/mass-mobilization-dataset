Bahrain — January 2022 research dossier (provisional)

Method and coverage
Thirteen actual search invocations are preserved in search_log.jsonl with full returned result headers and observed clocks. Queries covered English and Arabic, week-level windows (1–7, 8–14, 15–21, and 22–28 January), Bahrain-wide and village terms, Sitra and other localities, worker/labor and student actions, prison-related leads, English wire-service searches, and December/January and January/February boundaries. Searches included local Bahrain Mirror/Al-Wefaq reporting, Manama Post, the February 14 Coalition, rights organizations, and other news results. These searches are discovery coverage, not a complete archive census.

Nine source cards are retained: one article body was opened (BHR2022-01-S001), seven cards remain search-only, and one exact URL was blocked before body access (BHR2022-01-S009). The blocked page was not retried and no alternate route was used. One body read does not imply corroboration.

Candidate
BHR2022-01-E001 is the annual “Coming, O Sitra 7” march in Sitra, reported underway on 1 January. Manama Post attributes it to the Youth Coalition of February 14 Revolution and reports demands for self-determination, a new political system ensuring justice and equality, and release of political prisoners and prisoners of conscience. These are domestic political demands. The article calls participants peaceful and alleges that Ministry of Interior-affiliated civilian forces used tear gas to disperse the march. Both claims are attributed to this single opposition-aligned report. No attendance count or explicit qualitative size is given, so eligibility is pending_threshold. The physical end and any continuation are unknown. No official Mass Mobilization ID or approval is assigned.

Other leads and exclusions
BHR2022-01-X001 preserves a search-only report of Jaw Prison detainees refusing breakfast over prolonged isolation. It appears to describe a confined prison action, with no public gathering or attendance evidence, so it is provisionally excluded from public-gathering candidates; date and details remain unverified.

Search-result excerpts for Al-Wefaq/Bahrain Mirror report 16 protests in 9 areas during 8–14 January, 15 in 9 areas during 15–21 January, 16 in 11 areas during 22–28 January, and more than 84 peaceful protests across 28 villages in January, plus prison protests. The excerpts do not identify individual event dates and attendance. An AOHR UK result refers to five children detained for peaceful demonstration participation and escalating protests in Sitra; five is a detention count, not attendance, and the exact article URL returned HTTP 403 before body access. Neither lead establishes another January event meeting the public 50-person threshold.

Continuations and unresolved evidence
No event demonstrably beginning before January and continuing into the month was established; the 26 December organizer notice is pre-event promotion only. No verified continuation from January into February was found. Gaps G001–G007 preserve the unresolved candidate threshold, event boundary, response details, aggregate reports, confined prison lead, blocked rights report, and search-coverage limits. “Not found in these searches” does not mean “did not happen.”

Research remains provisional pending coordinator review and the official-baseline gate. No candidate has been added to or merged with the canonical Mass Mobilization catalogue.
