Burundi — January 2022 first-pass research

Outcome: no qualifying domestic state-targeted public protest assembly is supported by the reviewed evidence. events.json is empty; this is an open-web research judgment, not a finding that no protest occurred.

Coverage: 34 actual web.search invocations were captured as they occurred, with 31 source cards. Searches covered the full month and weekly windows, French and English queries, Kirundi and Kiswahili terms, major cities/provinces, and labor, education, market, fuel, transport, and rights leads. Results are discovery records; query count is not a measure of event coverage. Six sources were opened, including one partial IWACU body; most other source cards remain search-only. No comprehensive press archive, local-language hemeroteca, or official Mass Mobilization baseline was available for this pass.

Principal leads kept distinct:
- Teacher assessment: IWACU’s 15 January body reports a threatened future strike after talks failed. It documents no executed strike or public gathering. Ministry suspension and presidential-threat details are search-only; a February agreement is outside-month follow-up.
- Kwa Siyoni/Bujumbura City Market: contemporaneous bodies report a work stoppage beginning 25 January, with traders at closed stands. The collective demand is directed at private owner/management. The reported 5,000-plus traders is the market population, not turnout. A tax-cost reference does not establish a collective state-policy demand. No public assembly, participant count, exact physical end, or state response is established.
- Ntega: an opened report describes objections to a proposed local contribution for substitute-teacher pay, but no implemented protest or public assembly.
- Former Bujumbura Central Market: a 27 January RPA search result describes public frustration with unfulfilled government reconstruction promises. It identifies a possible state-policy grievance but no January protest action, assembly, or attendance; it is not merged with the private Kwa Siyoni rent dispute.
- School transformer, fuel shortage, forced porterage, presidential Q&A, and association-meeting leads do not establish qualifying protest assemblies in the available search evidence.

Open gaps include the pending official baseline, incomplete local-language and province coverage, technical retrieval failures on exact ABP/IWACU/Jimbere URLs, missing participant and event-form evidence for the market stoppage, uncertainty about any executed teacher action, and incomplete follow-up on the Ntega contribution proposal. Failed CacheMiss/HTTP 503 attempts followed by ordinary exact-URL DNS failures are recorded as technical gaps, not publisher denials.

No IDs from the official dataset, approvals, or consolidated records are asserted. Prepared as a worker draft for root review; no canonical files or Git state were changed.
