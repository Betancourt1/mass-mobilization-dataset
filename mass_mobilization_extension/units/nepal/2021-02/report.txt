Nepal — February 2021 open-web research dossier

Scope and method
This is a first-pass research dossier for Nepal, 2021-02, using the inherited public-gathering criteria and event cutoff of 2026-10-07 (America/Mexico_City). Search was conducted in English and Nepali through 18 actual batched web-search actions. The durable search log preserves every original action, query batch, ordered result header and URL: 599 headers total. A separate extractor check against the assigned runtime rollout matched all 18 original event IDs, query texts, ordered headers and URL arrays position by position. Searches are a discovery aid, not evidence of event occurrence or a census.

The open-web workflow differs from systematic LexisNexis/archive searching. No claim of exhaustive local or national coverage is made. Search results included national reporting, local leads, rights-sector sources and retrospective/contextual reports, but only one contemporary article body was read. The official baseline and comparison to the original manual remain pending coordinator review.

Candidate inventory
Twelve candidate or discovery leads are saved in events.json. Only the February 12 Women’s March has a body-supported proposed inclusion. The opened Himalayan Times article describes hundreds of women’s-rights activists and supporters in Kathmandu, calls for equality and an end to violence and discrimination, and reports that police barriers blocked the march toward the Prime Minister’s Office. The article explicitly reports no clashes or violence. The exact attendance and physical end are unknown. Crowd dispersal is coded yes for the documented route blockade; the other six state-response dimensions remain unknown unless separately evidenced. The proposal is not an official approval.

Other candidates remain pending: the February 4 Dahal–Nepal faction strike-related lead; February 6 citizen-led action; Bhatta-related student, far-west, and vigil leads; February 10 faction rallies/mass-gathering; a Badi sit-in with unresolved start and continuation; the February 23 Indigenous-group rally; post-court and Chitwan rally leads; a possible planned youth rally; and a possible pro-Oli support rally. Search-only headline terms such as “mass,” “thousands,” and “huge” were not converted into attendance estimates. Strike impacts, arrests, property damage, and a government agreement were not treated as crowd evidence or protest-response coding. A scheduled protest is not coded as held. Potential partisan support actions remain unresolved rather than being automatically included or excluded.

Continuity and exclusions
One possible continuation note flags the February 6 citizen-led movement title alongside Nepal January candidate nepal_2021-01_E002. The prior January source card records the January 30 start and was consulted locally; no February article body was reopened. The February title does not establish one uninterrupted episode, so no merge was made. The Badi sit-in may continue into March, but the start date and uninterrupted episode are unresolved. No exclusion met a sufficiently verified threshold in this first pass, so excluded.json is an empty array; uncertain and possible support-only leads remain visible in events.json and gaps.json.

Source access and limits
There are 34 source cards: one February body-opened report, one previously opened January source consulted locally through its saved card and scope, and 32 search-only cards. A fresh receipt/manual-review chain preceded the single successful body read. The article scope was lines 73–93; no images were assessed. The Kathmandu Post February 4 article was attempted once after its own fresh receipt, complete manual review, saved note readback/hash and immediate input check. The request timed out before HTTP; the corrected outcome retains the native request interval, reports no HTTP status, and records zero body reads. It is a technical retrieval failure, not a denial. Three failed local preflight chains and the February 12 preflight that detected changed corpus hashes are recorded as no-request outcomes; superseded file bytes remain in access_receipts history. No retry or alternate-host fallback was used.

The records distinguish search-only leads from body-backed facts, preserve all seven response dimensions, use nulls where evidence is absent, and keep search coverage, baseline reconciliation, source independence, attendance thresholds, date precision, event ownership, physical endings, and most event-specific responses open for coordinator review. No official source IDs or catalogue changes were made.
