Nepal, January 2021 — open-web first pass pending review

This non-exhaustive first pass covers January 1–31, 2021, for provisional ccode 790, queue 109. The official baseline remains unresolved. 12 literal searches are logged, using English and Nepali queries. The dossier contains 17 candidate leads, 1 documented exclusion, 0 continuations, 24 follow-up gaps, and 58 unique source URLs (19 opened, including coordinator-access records, and 39 search-only). Candidate totals are not unique confirmed events. The open-web process does not reproduce LexisNexis or other systematic archives.

Candidates include the Jan. 22 Dahal–Nepal faction rally in Kathmandu; the Jan. 30 citizen-led demonstration; Jan. 21 torch rallies and a Butwal rally; Jan. 20 Nepali Congress ward-level actions; Jan. 9 and Jan. 16 Nepali Congress programs; Jan. 10 student-wing bullock-cart actions and a possible separate Maitighar clash; the Jan. 25 Broad Citizens Movement march and a separate rights-defender gathering; the Jan. 29 Nepali Congress protest at Singha Durbar; the Jan. 31 Sifal civic protest; a Jan. 16 Janata Samajwadi Party demonstration in Rautahat described as drawing “scores”; the Jan. 6 ANNFSU lantern rally and nationwide chapter whistle rallies; a Jan. 14 Nepal Student Union demonstration; and a Jan. 11 RPP-led monarchy-restoration march. Most remain unresolved on attendance, event identity, state target, response, or physical ending. The Jan. 9 Oli-aligned supportive rally is recorded as excluded under the political-rally rule.

For Jan. 22, Al Jazeera attributes a 25,000-person estimate to a senior police official, while a separate Kathmandu Post story says “thousands attended.” The reports establish a large gathering, but 25,000 remains an attributed estimate rather than an exact count. The Al Jazeera web-reader page was originally recorded as opened. A coordinator’s later ordinary-HTTPS check returned HTTP 404; that separate check does not overwrite the original access record. The Jan. 20 figure of 6,743 refers to ward locations, not people. The Jan. 9 SATP timeline attributes a report to THT describing “thousands” across all 330 provincial constituencies and a mass at Budhanilkanta, without venue-level counts; nationwide-versus-local event grouping remains unresolved. Jan. 16 reporting of actions at 753 local levels likewise does not give venue-level attendance.

For Jan. 25, contemporary Kathmandu Post reporting documents batons, water-cannon dispersal, and conflicting injury accounts during the Broad Citizens Movement march. Police reported five protesters and five security personnel injured and treated; campaigners reported more than a dozen protesters injured and six still hospitalized. The Jan. 26 follow-up quotes a police spokesperson denying arrests during the civil-society march. The official NHRC statement broadly refers to police arrests during a Jan. 25 protest without distinguishing the earlier rights-defender gathering from the later march, so march-specific arrests remain unknown. The separate rights-defender gathering is reported to have had around two dozen detentions; this is not treated as a crowd estimate. No crowd count or physical end is established for either gathering.

The opened Kathmandu Post Jan. 6 report describes the ANNFSU lantern rally in Kathmandu and chapter whistle rallies across the country; it gives no site-level counts, event-specific response, or physical end. The opened Republica report confirms a Jan. 14 NSU demonstration in Kathmandu but does not quantify it. SATP’s opened January timeline is secondary: it describes a Jan. 10 Maitighar bullock-cart clash that may be the Kathmandu component of a multi-city action, and a Jan. 11 monarchy-restoration march followed by police directions to use pavements, refusal, and a clash. The latter does not establish a specific force mechanism. An opened THT report says Nepali Congress cadres protested at Singha Durbar on Jan. 29 and were arrested but gives neither attendance nor arrest count. Two exact-phrase searches for the Jan. 9 THT report did not find it; timeline variants and a reprint are not independent confirmation.

Searches surfaced Jan. 12 and Jan. 26 planned rallies and a Jan. 27 plan for Feb. 1 protests; these remain plans absent independent occurrence evidence. Official NHRC statements reviewed for Jan. 20 and 21 concern partial implementation of a Dec. 28 sugarcane-payment agreement and Ganga Maya Adhikari’s individual fast begun Dec. 21, 2020; neither establishes a new qualifying January mass assembly. A Jan. 30 article’s reference to repeated protests since Dec. 20 does not establish a single continuous physical event. No cross-month continuation is established.
