Austria — October 2020 research dossier

Scope and method

This dossier covers only Austria, 2020-10, in the assigned 2020Q4 unit. Searches used German and English across full-month and weekly windows, with Vienna and regional Austrian terms and sector/topic searches for labor and care, education, climate and transport, COVID measures, asylum, racism, monuments, gender violence, and cross-month continuity. The work used the managed public search service and stored each actual submitted query in search_log.jsonl with the response headers captured by the tool. Search date filters were unreliable: opened July 2020 and January 2021 results demonstrate that the engine returned out-of-month material despite dated queries.

There are 37 query records and 536 retained result headers. Seven submitted queries have outputlost/unknown headers: the original three-query batch and a later four-query batch whose shared response could not be preserved in full. Their empty header arrays mean unknown/lost output, not no results. Two individual queries returned explicit empty-search responses. Search captures preserve returned title, URL, reference and order where available; native tool clocks were not supplied and remain null.

The unit has 41 source cards: 29 cards have current-unit body-read evidence, 9 remain search-only, and 3 are blocked on source-specific restrictions. Source metadata records 29 current-unit body-read cards; prior-unit-only body opens: 0. One additional follow-up body response is excluded from those counts because it occurred before a fresh per-attempt precheck. Each other attempted body access has a unique receipt in access_receipts/. S007’s managed open returned HTTP 403 and was not retried; its limitation is restricted to that exact source. S020 was refused by robots.txt and S022 returned HTTP 403 on their exact URLs; neither restriction was generalized. S038 returned a tool-level “not accessible” error with no HTTP status, recorded as technical failure rather than a denial. Search-only cards and announcements do not count as proof of an event or turnout.

One procedure anomaly is disclosed: an S015 follow-up open at line 74 happened before a fresh receipt was made for that follow-up. That earlier response is not used. A unique exact-URL access-check receipt was then saved and the page reopened; only the latter body evidence supports the S015 card and October 23 climate candidate. Candidate records

Seven event candidates are proposed for inclusion on current evidence:

- 3 October, Vienna “Wir haben Platz”: a public march calling on Austria to admit refugees. ORF Vienna, APA-attributed reporting in Der Standard, the contemporaneous organizer call, and the CBA chronology identify 3 October. ORF’s national item instead says “yesterday” relative to 3 October publication, implying 2 October; that conflict is retained. Attendance is reported as several hundred initially / more than 1,200 in CBA, with organizers estimating a peak up to 5,000; no police estimate is available.
- 3 October, Vienna care and social-workforce rally: CBA’s chronology reports about 210 people; the event listing explicitly directs its funding demand to the federal, state and municipal public authorities and asks for more staffing, shorter hours and higher wages.
- 18 October, Salzburg anti-COVID-measures rally: police estimate 350, organizers 750. The event opposed federal and Salzburg state measures; some called for government resignation.
- 19 October, Innsbruck student/parent/teacher demonstration: ORF Tirol reports more than 100 against Tyrol’s upper-secondary distance-learning policy. The education councillor attended, defended the rule and promised an evaluation after three weeks.
- 23 October, Vienna climate-just-mobility blockade at the Industriellenvereinigung: ORF reports around 100 and quotes organizers saying they would not wait for state and federal governments to take the mobility transition seriously. The immediate blockade target was the industry association; the quoted own-state policy demand is why it remains proposed. ORF’s article is agency credited; the organizer figure comes from a republished interview with a written Mosaik-Blog origin, not an independent count.
- 26 October, Vienna anti-COVID-measures demonstration: police-reported about 1,500. A separate counterdemonstration of about 140 is not pooled. Police, city health officials and federal ministries discussed responsibility for compliance and dispersal; Vienna health authorities allowed the demonstration to continue.
- 31 October, Vienna Heldenplatz anti-COVID-measures rally: police described a fluctuating “few hundred”; 15,000 was the announced/registered figure, not attendance. Police reported 11 administrative citations. This is a separate event from 26 October.

The 15 October MAN Steyr warning strike and city-square public march are retained as pending_state_target, not excluded: oe24/APA reports at least 4,000 by the works council; GPA reports around 5,000. Opened sources show that the main explicit demand targeted MAN/VW management and owners to keep the plant. GPA also criticized the Chancellor and minister for not returning a works-council call, and an Upper Austrian official attended and pledged state-level efforts, but a specific protester demand to the Austrian state is not yet clear. The 2,300 affected jobs are not attendance.

Other pending candidates preserve unresolved eligibility rather than implying absence: the 1 October #HassIstNichtNormal call has demands but no verified occurrence or turnout; the Lueger-monument Schandwache began on 5 October with clear demands to Vienna city leadership but no simultaneous crowd count or final day; the femicide demonstration has about-100 estimates but conflicting 5–7 October dates and no explicit government measure in the opened post; the 7 October anti-racism entry lacks turnout and target details; the 9 October FPÖ-rally counterprotest lacks crowd and demand details; the 10 October Belarus solidarity event lacks crowd and an Austrian-state target; the scheduled 2 October highway-project climate strike and 9 October student-security demonstration lack verified actual attendance/occurrence. These remain separate and are not merged by topic or campaign label.

Three exclusions are documented: the 3 October “population exchange” far-right march/counteraction lacks a documented Austrian-government policy demand in the opened chronology; a search false positive for an ORF women’s march was actually dated 10 July 2020; and an ORF multi-city university-law protest was actually dated 12 January 2021. The false positives are out of month. The chronology’s far-right item remains subject to its stated coverage limitations.

Continuity and coverage gaps

CBA records Rebellion Week from 26 September through 4 October, including decentralized climate actions and road blockades; it began before the assigned month and specific October-day turnout is unavailable, so it is recorded as cross-month continuity and not automatically merged with the separate 23 October IV blockade. The 11–24 October Freie Radios chronology (S007) could not be opened because the managed tool returned HTTP 403; this leaves a documented late-month coverage gap. The source itself was not retried.

Other explicit gaps cover the unconfirmed October 2 and 9 scheduled actions; the October 1/7 racism-action relation; the femicide date and state target; the Schandwache’s crowd/end date; the MAN state-target distinction; unresolved turnout for the October 1, 7, 9 and 10 events; seven lost search responses; and the unreceipted S015 follow-up anomaly. See gaps.json for source-linked detail.

This is open-web research, not a claim of exhaustive October mobilization. Structural validation can establish only dossier consistency. Official baseline/version and exact Austria country-code mapping were not verified; baseline_gate remains pending_official_baseline, and no official protest IDs or approval are asserted.
