Suriname — January 2021 provisional open-web research

Research status: researched open web, pending coordinator review. The fixed event cutoff is 2026-10-07 inclusive in America/Mexico_City. The official baseline gate remains pending; no official Mass Mobilization IDs or approval are claimed.

Methods and coverage

Twenty single-query web searches were recorded with their actual UTC execution times. English and Dutch queries covered the four January weekly windows (1–7, 8–14, 15–21, and 22–31), followed by targeted searches for Pique-led actions, labor and education, farming/land/housing/environment, regional districts, Sranan-related terms, and continuity before and after January. The January-date operators were not assumed to filter results reliably: search results often included 2022–2025 pages, which were excluded from January event coding after checking dates where relevant. Search output did not provide a stable complete result total; no total is invented and result counts are not used as a measure of coverage.

The Sranan-related query used terms such as “aksi” and “pina” but returned mostly Dutch pages already surfaced by other searches. Broad Sranan Tongo coverage was not established. Targeted labor, student, agriculture, land, and regional searches did not surface additional verified January 2021 gatherings, but these searches do not establish that none occurred. Open-web indexing is not equivalent to the historical LexisNexis source set, and this is not an exhaustive month census.

Candidates

SUR202101-E001 records the 9 January gathering led by Sibrano Pique in Paramaribo. Waterkant reports that the group grew to about 200 at Independence Square and that participants later left after Pique thanked them; its 10 January follow-up gives 100–200 for the Saturday action. Starnieuws describes the turnout as “dozens.” These reports differ in precision; the Waterkant accounts are from one outlet and are not independent counts. The action opposed Santokhi government policy, called for the president to leave, and included an “Stop the IMF deal” sign. Waterkant reports that the group wanted to speak with an Assembly member and that chair Marinus Bee offered a conversation for the following Monday, but the meeting is not shown as completed. The group’s physical departure on 9 January is reported; other response dimensions remain unknown. The candidate is pending unit ownership because Starnieuws mentions earlier Pique actions without dates or attendance.

SUR202101-E002 records the 20 January protest and petition presentation. Waterkant reports about 100 attendees, compared with pre-event projections of about 500; the projection is not treated as observed turnout. It reports demands concerning the IMF agreement, civil servants without pay or sent home, high prices, and the exchange rate. Assembly chair Marinus Bee accepted the petition and spoke briefly, without evidence that the demands were granted. Before the gathering, the district commissioner refused the permit, urged Pique not to protest, and warned that participants would face consequences if they gathered. This is coded as an attributed attempt to deter the gathering, not as successful dispersal: Waterkant reports that about 100 people attended. A 23 January arrest at Pique’s home under a narcotics-law allegation is recorded separately and is not attributed to this protest. The candidate is pending unit ownership because its relationship to the 9 January Pique action is unresolved.

A Monday follow-up was announced after 9 January. A blocked GFC Nieuws headline says a Monday protest action was called off, but its date cannot be established and it is not assumed to refer to 11 January. The available sources do not establish that a 11 January gathering occurred. A 20 January protest is directly reported and is not represented as an announcement-only lead.

Continuity, exclusions, and sources

One earlier-context record preserves Starnieuws’s undated account that Pique had led actions since the government took office. A September 2020 Waterkant article documents a protest call about fuel prices and the exchange rate, but not the event’s occurrence or turnout. It does not establish a qualifying earlier gathering or one continuous episode through January. A separate 19 February Starnieuws report describes another Pique-related gathering at a police post; attendance is not reported, and no continuous physical episode from January is established.

One source-only exclusion preserves the GFC headline about a called-off Monday protest. Its body returned an access denial, and the date and link to any January announcement remain unknown.

The source file contains 21 exact-URL records: 10 opened, 8 blocked, and 3 search-only. Opened Dutch-language reporting supplies most event evidence. Search-result headers were retained for relevant unopened pages, without treating those headers as confirmation of event details. Starnieuws’s 10 January account and Waterkant’s 10 January report are related for the planned Monday action: Waterkant explicitly attributes that announcement to an interview with Starnieuws. Duplicate or same-publisher pages are not counted as independent corroboration.

Access limitations

Ordinary exact-URL requests were attempted only after web-tool Cache miss/Internal Error or local DNS failure. GFC Nieuws, SR Herald’s January 18 permit story, Waterkant’s January 18 planned-action story, Shoeket’s petition page, SR Herald’s January 9 page, Waterkant’s linked “circa 100” video page, and ABC’s NDP-orchestration article returned HTTP 403. De West’s January 20 page returned HTTP 406 from Sucuri. A CDS government page could not be verified because its TLS certificate had expired. These denials were not retried or bypassed. The YouTube page returned HTTP 200; only its title and description metadata were inspected, not the video. Where a blocked page’s date appears in its URL path, that date is not represented as a separately observed publication timestamp.

The saved May 2015 manual is historical and the additional Coding Procedure remains unavailable. The current official dataset version, universe, schema, and reuse conditions remain unverified. Candidates remain provisional and are neither merged into the integrated catalogue nor assigned official IDs. This work is not independent human double-coding and does not claim exhaustive coverage.

Serialization and file validation

The first source/status rewrite command exited 0 but wrote literal backslash-n text instead of LF separators. The coordinator’s independent status parse exited 1; a worker diagnostic printed the source JSONDecodeError but exited 0 because it caught the exception. The repair exited 0 after reloading and preserving all 21 source records and rewriting actual LF separators with chr(10). A follow-up schema check found that the 445 captured title/URL/web_ref headers were present under notes.returned_results but lacked a top-level returned_results alias. They were momentarily moved to top level, which caused the coordinator’s nested-array verification to return KeyError with exit 1; no header data was lost. I restored notes.returned_results as an exact copy of the top-level returned_results arrays for all 20 queries, preserving both copies, all notes, each query and the ordered returned_urls projection. The restoration exited 0 and used the saved captured arrays; no search was rerun. Final validation confirmed both arrays are identical for every query and match the original captures and returned_urls order. A later hash-check script also exited 0 but reported a composite mismatch because its calculation used an escaped delimiter; the corrected LF calculation was checked again after the final file freeze.

Resource state

No persistent server, browser, container, or other background resource was started. Every direct HTTPS retrieval command ended with numeric exit 0; the two delayed command sessions were polled to numeric exit 0. No persistent process remains.
