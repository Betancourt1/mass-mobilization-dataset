Four provisional leads remain in events.json. The Jan 6 Dakar curfew action is reported across neighborhoods; Nettali describes large numbers in some neighborhoods and says actions were coordinated, but gives no attendance count or scale tied to 50 at one meeting. AFP reports in VOA and Channels share one origin; some local detail is attributed to L’AS or EnQuête. Tear gas used to disperse groups is explicit; protester tire burning, barriers and projectiles are attributed to some actors. No physical end is established.

In Mbour, opened MetroDakar reporting and a SenePlus interview support local curfew-night activity and an objection to policy, but no count establishes 50. Leral’s headline-only arrest claim remains uncoded.

Opened Senego and Teranga News reporting documents a Ziguinchor protest over public lighting on the Boulevard des 54 m. Teranga News dates it to the night of Jan 14–15 and reports several dozen young people across Néma 2, Kandialang, Tilène and other areas, tire burning, and nineteen detentions followed by release. Senego reports four arrests but says “Thursday 14 December” despite its Jan 14 publication date; this conflict remains explicit. Several other headlines give different arrest totals; they are not summed and are not turnout. “Several dozen” does not establish 50 at one meeting. The public-service demand has no clear fit in the original seven categories, so no category is assigned. Arrests are coded yes; release and a future repair promise are not coded as accommodation. Other response dimensions remain unknown unless specifically evidenced.

A planned Jan 29 Tambacounda march to demand a regional university was prohibited by the prefect and cancelled, according to an opened local report quoting an organizer. The group planned flyer distribution and decided to hand a memorandum to the governor that day; the report does not confirm either activity was completed. No qualifying public gathering is documented, so the lead is retained in excluded.json. A separate July 2021 march by the same group, reported at about one hundred people in APS-derived copies, is outside January. The Feb 10 EnQuête issue likewise describes later Sonko-affair protest activity, not a continuation of the January curfew or public-lighting actions. No qualifying public-meeting continuation from December into the Jan 6 curfew or the reported Jan 14–15 lighting action is evidenced. A separate road-transport strike began Dec 25 and ended Jan 3–4, but no qualifying public meeting is documented; it remains excluded and is not coded as a continuation. This does not prove no other continuation.
