Singapore, January 2021 — open-web research first pass
Finished: 2026-10-09T02:43:35Z

Scope and method
Research covered Singapore events dated 1–31 January 2021. The search log preserves 13 actual web queries with their literal query text, call-time timestamps, and returned title/URL/citation-reference headers in order. Five disjoint weekly windows were searched explicitly: January 1–7 (Q002), 8–14 (Q003), 15–21 (Q004), 22–28 (Q009), and 29–31 (Q010). Q005 is separately retained as a broad January 22–31 sweep. English discovery was supplemented with Chinese (Q006), Malay (Q007), and Tamil (Q008) terms. Q011 checked a conflicting date/participant-count lead; Q012 looked back into December 2020; Q013 checked February 2021 for episode continuity. Returned result counts and saved URL totals describe tool output only; they are not evidence of exhaustive coverage.

Finding and disposition
One concrete January physical assembly surfaced repeatedly: a five-person protest outside the Ministry of Education headquarters on Buona Vista Drive on 26 January, reported at about 5pm. The Singapore Police Force release, CNA, News24, and the Chinese-language Lianhe Zaobao article report five attendees. CNA attributes the group’s demand to end discrimination against LGBTQ+ students in schools to a group statement directed to Education Minister Lawrence Wong. Since five is below the protocol’s at-least-50 public-assembly threshold, the action is recorded in excluded.json rather than events.json. No invented turnout estimate or official event ID is assigned. Later police investigation and warning counts are not treated as the number assembled.

The contemporary sources say two of the five left after security staff intervened; the remaining three were warned, issued a move-on direction, and arrested around 5:35pm. Those reports concern the same 26 January action; later November coverage is legal follow-up, not another gathering. The January search found no evidence that this physical assembly continued from December or into February. February reporting about Myanmar-related planned demonstrations and a separately reported embassy action concerns a different place and issue, so it is not recorded as a continuation. The adjacent-month searches remain open-web checks, not proof that no unreported activity existed.

Evidence and source relationships
The official police release directly reports the date, place, five-person group and police action. CNA reports the same police account and includes the group’s statement; its article credits TODAY for some participant remarks. News24’s opened report also relays police information and quotes a participant, but its wire-agency provenance was not visible. Lianhe Zaobao supplied a directly read Chinese-language report and likewise discusses the police account. These outlets do not constitute independent corroboration of every police-response detail. Search-result-only URLs remain marked search_only and are not used as factual support.

A current Wikipedia page says “6 January 2021” and six people, but cites later November police-warning reporting. This conflicts with the opened contemporary SPF, CNA, News24, and Lianhe Zaobao accounts, which give 26 January and five. The discrepancy is retained as a contested lead; no cause is inferred and it does not change the exclusion.

Access and limitations
The Lianhe Zaobao page first returned a web-tool Cache miss. One ordinary HTTPS request to the same public URL returned HTTP 200; selected article paragraphs were read in memory and no article copy was saved. Web-tool Cache misses for The Straits Times’ January article, Malay Mail, and an Asian human-rights page left those records search_only; no alternate route was used. The Jakarta Post URL returned an actual 403 and was not retried. A February CNA article cited by FIDH returned HTTP 404 on one ordinary HTTPS request; the exact URL and citation provenance are retained as not_found, with no retry. The web tool opened the FIDH PDF and the February CNA/Straits Times warning coverage; only relevant sections were read.

Open-web searches do not reproduce LexisNexis or local newspaper archive access. Chinese discovery produced one directly read local-language report; Malay and Tamil searches did not identify a directly read report on a different January gathering. Search ranking, indexing, translation and archive coverage remain uneven. The official current dataset bytes, universe, schema and reuse conditions, as well as the additional Coding Procedure, remain unresolved at the coordinator’s baseline gate. This file is a research proposal for review, not an official catalogue update or an exhaustive absence claim.

No server, watcher, browser automation, container, VM, or persistent request process was started. All finite retrieval/check commands exited in the foreground; the one default-sandbox DNS failure and the subsequent one-shot ordinary HTTPS request are recorded above.
