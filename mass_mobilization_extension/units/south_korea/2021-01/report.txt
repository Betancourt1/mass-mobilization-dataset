South Korea — January 2021 open-web research dossier

The unit covers events physically beginning January 1–31, 2021, using the fixed research cutoff of October 7, 2026, inclusive, in America/Mexico_City. The official event baseline and complete current coding procedure remain unavailable, so this is a research dossier for review rather than an approved update.

I ran 24 English- and Korean-language searches across the January weeks, with additional regional and sector queries and checks for November 2020 and February 2021 railway continuity. All 24 queries, execution timestamps, and 507 returned title/URL/reference headers are persisted in search_log.jsonl. Returned URL arrays match captured result headers positionally. The search engine repeatedly surfaced later-2021 material despite January terms; dates were checked in opened pages. This search count is not evidence of exhaustive coverage.

Seven dated physical actions are recorded as candidates: the Jan. 6 Busan fitness-association press conference; a separate Jan. 6 Busan screen-golf owners' protest; Korail Networks-related press conferences at Wonju Station on Jan. 4 and by civil-society organizations on Jan. 12; a Seoul Station sit-in whose Jan. 9 start is derived from a source calling Jan. 11 its third day; coordinated Jan. 15 strike-victory rallies at Seoul, Daejeon, Gwangju Songjeong, and Busan stations; and an Itaewon merchants' press conference on Jan. 9. All remain pending review. No candidate has event-specific evidence that definitively establishes at least 50 attendees. The Jan. 6 fitness figure is approximately 50 in S419, a Dong-A Ilbo article by Kim So-young and Lee Ji-woon; News1 is credited only for a photo/caption, and no firm lower bound is established. S419 gives no count for the screen-golf protest; News1 is credited only for a photo/caption. A Chosun search-result excerpt says about 50, but the article body was not readable and the count is not treated as verified. The Jan. 12 civic press conference also has an unresolved state target.

The Nov. 11, 2020 Korail Networks and Railway Customer Center all-member strike is recorded separately as an older-onset continuation. Yonhap reports that the all-member strike ended with return to work from 3 p.m. on Jan. 15 and shifted to a cadre strike. The end of the broader campaign is not established. The approximately 1,000 strike-wide participants reported by Yonhap are not assigned to any January gathering.

Six actions are retained as exclusions: nine coin-karaoke owners' press conference; cafe owners' relay one-person demonstrations; an announced Gwangju sign-lighting action at separate bar/nightclub premises (execution unconfirmed); the Constitutional Court petition; an online petition; and business reopening without a documented mass gathering. These do not establish a qualifying gathering of at least 50.

All seven state-response dimensions and protester violence remain unknown for every January candidate because the cited sources do not establish event-specific state actions. A Jan. 10 Yonhap report documents Busan's partial reopening of indoor sports facilities from Jan. 11 and notes recent protests, but does not link the decision to the specific Jan. 6 association. This is preserved as an unresolved response question, not coded as accommodation.

The 15 retained source cards include 13 opened pages, one search-only Chosun result, and one HTTP-403-blocked disability-rights page. S418 and S420 are separately attributed News1-origin reports. S419 is by Dong-A Ilbo reporters Kim So-young and Lee Ji-woon, with News1 credited only for a photo/caption; its written-reporting relationship to S418 and S420 is not established, so no automatic independent crowd corroboration is claimed. The OhmyNews Jan. 28 labor report also appeared on Newscham under the same reporter and is one reporting origin. A separate MK result initially appeared to target January but redirected to an October 20, 2021 article; it is preserved as a wrong-month access result and excluded from January evidence. The 2021 and 2020 KRWU chronologies and contemporary OhmyNews/Yonhap reports support the railway chronology and the November start date.

English/Korean discovery covered Seoul, Busan, Gwangju, Daejeon, and Wonju and focused on business owners and railway labor. Other regions and sectors remain under-covered. A disability-rights page returned HTTP 403 and was not retried or bypassed. Open-web searching cannot establish absence; LexisNexis coverage, the official baseline, official IDs, and independent human double-coding remain unresolved.

Process and access record: the exact Yonhap Jan. 6 URL and the MK search-result URL each had one ordinary HTTPS request fail at DNS (numeric process exit 1). Each exact URL was then requested once through the authorized network path and returned HTTP 200 with numeric process exit 0. Yonhap returned its requested URL and its article/caption were read. MK redirected to https://www.mk.co.kr/news/society/10067300, dated Oct. 20, 2021, and was excluded. The Chosun open call returned only a one-line header and remains search-only; the Librewiki page returned HTTP 403 and was not retried. Local file writes and the final structural validation returned numeric exit 0 with no session_id. One earlier validation returned numeric exit 1 because the source count had not yet been updated; two malformed status-update commands each returned numeric exit 1 without changing files; both commands were corrected. Web tool calls do not expose local process exits. No persistent server, watcher, browser automation, container, or other temporary resource was started.

The dossier remains open for independent review. The baseline gate is pending, event eligibility is not approved, and no official dataset or public contribution was changed.
