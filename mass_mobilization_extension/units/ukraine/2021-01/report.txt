Ukraine — January 2021 research report
Status: researched_open_web_pending_review. This is a candidate dossier, not an official Mass Mobilization update.

Method and coverage
Fourteen open-web queries were run in Ukrainian, Russian and English: broad January tariff/energy/protest searches, weekly windows, regional/city follow-ups, a labor/strike search, and December 2020/February 2021 continuity searches. The log preserves all 307 captured result headers in provider order; search providers exposed no total. I retained 114 relevant source cards: 11 bodies opened, 5 exact URLs blocked, and 98 search-only leads. A result-header count is not a coverage measure. No search-only excerpt is used as event proof. The query log retains title-capture limitations for generic PDF headings, newspaper mastheads and truncated fragments, with uncaptured titles null and URL/ref order preserved.

Open-web search does not reproduce LexisNexis or systematic local archives. The official current dataset bytes/universe/schema, supplemental Coding Procedure and data-specific reuse terms remain unresolved; this unit assigns no official IDs and makes no baseline change.

Candidates
Sixteen date-specific candidates are recorded: 2 proposed_include, 9 pending_threshold, 3 pending_state_target and 2 pending_unit. These remain worker proposals.

For Jan. 10 Kharkiv, Suspilne reports a police-attributed regional total above 1,000 across the city and nearly 20 settlements (S094). Local reports include about 50 in Vysokyi (S091), about 50 in Balakliia and more than 70 in Vovchansk (S094). S091 and S094 share the same police origin. Site counts are not added to the regional total; the Liubotyn petition signers are not attendance, and its separate 15 roadblockers are excluded below threshold.

For Jan. 11, ICTV reports about 200 at the Poltava Regional State Administration over gas/gas-transmission tariffs and electricity preferences (S014), proposed for inclusion. The same report gives an unallocated total of about 170 across three Chernivtsi-region sites (S014), kept pending unit. The report is explicitly credited to Interfax-Ukraine; that does not make it independent corroboration.

Zhytomyr evidence distinguishes Jan. 10, 13 and 15 actions. A later Ukrinform report says an earlier Jan. 13 roadblocking action occurred and links the corresponding report (S043, S007). That report estimates up to 300 with no lower bound, describes a roughly 15-minute roadblock, and says protesters dispersed when traffic resumed. Officials spoke with participants and discussed an extraordinary council session. The separate Jan. 15 moving action across utility and government sites began at 11:00, ended after 14:00, and has no crowd estimate; officials spoke and a council appeal was reported (S043). Same-publisher items are not independent confirmation.

On Jan. 15, Radio Svoboda estimates about 150 at a Dnipro tariff protest beneath the regional administration, demanding the prime minister’s resignation and lower gas/electricity prices (S029), proposed for inclusion. A separate Jan. 24 action near Dnipro city council had several dozen participants and remains below threshold evidence (S030). The report says council members were absent because it was Sunday; this is not coded as intentional ignore. Suspilne reports more than 50 in Kropyvnytskyi, several dozen in Cherkasy, and about 50 in Odesa (S005); threshold and/or state-target questions remain. Vinnytsia Jan. 24 is described as large-scale and an appeal to the President/Prime Minister is reported, but there is no crowd count (S070). The separate Jan. 25 report gives more than 100 and calls the gathering peaceful, but does not establish its government addressee (S070).

The Jan. 28 Kyiv FOP rally/march is a boundary record (C001), not assigned a proved January onset. S006 reports about one thousand at Independence Square and a march to official/diplomatic sites, and says Tax Maidan had continued since Dec. 15. This does not establish uninterrupted physical presence or whether Jan. 28 was a phase or distinct assembly. The reported campaign date is kept distinct from physical episode onset.

State response and continuity
Only actual official engagement in the two Zhytomyr actions supports accommodation. Unreported response dimensions remain unknown. Silence is not ignore; police presence, traffic handling, participant blockades and security preventing entry do not by themselves prove state crowd dispersal. No source establishes arrests, beatings, killings or shootings at these candidates. The main open boundaries are crowd threshold, explicit state target, Kharkiv/Chernivtsi aggregation, Zhytomyr date-specific actions, Vezha’s missing event year, and Tax Maidan’s physical continuity. A February search returned no February gathering header, which is not evidence that no February gathering occurred. Thirteen follow-up gaps are recorded.

Data handling
No article body was retained in the repository; no contact or publication occurred; no long-lived temporary resource was started. Two earlier source-card writer subprocesses returned exit 1 after atomic replacement because a print branch lacked a sys import. Whole-file readback and hash checks verified the bytes afterward, but both observed exits remain exit 1, not retroactive success. The import was fixed before later writes. Other first-attempt title/serialization/validation errors occurred before mutation and were corrected without rerunning original evidence queries.
