Malawi — January 2021 research (provisional; open-web review)

Scope and method
Research is limited to Malawi, 1–31 January 2021. Search-log queries include generic protest, demonstration, strike and march terms; COVID restrictions; weekly early-January coverage; health/education, labor, transport, business and sex-worker topics; and English/Chichewa variants. Searches were followed by event-specific reporting and before/after-month checks. Search results were pooled by the web tool, so relevant URLs are recorded at batch level when query-to-URL mapping was unavailable. No query quota or result count is treated as proof of completeness.

Current candidate leads
- Six candidate records are retained for review: five with reports of actual gatherings and one announced AHL employee demonstration whose occurrence is unverified. The verified reports concern returnees at Mapanga (Jan 10), CDEDI and small-business operators in Blantyre (Jan 19), female sex workers in Zomba (Jan 20), minibus operators in multiple districts/cities (Jan 26), and FSWA in Lilongwe (Jan 28).
- The AHL plan was announced for Jan 4, but Jan 4 is not established as an event date: the start date remains unknown. The archive announcement is search-only, and no follow-up confirms whether a demonstration occurred. The demand’s target (employer versus state) is unresolved. Separate reporting describes salary arrears over a longer period but does not date an executed gathering.
- The Zomba article record remains search-only in this worker’s source log after a cache miss and DNS failure. The coordinator separately recovered the page over ordinary HTTPS and confirmed it describes an actual Jan 20 street action, turned back by city officials who asked for written concerns. No attendance count was reported.
- Returnees from South Africa protested quarantine at Mapanga Prison Training College and the nearby road on Jan 10; reports describe roadblocks, vehicle damage, a fire, police tear gas and an official passport response. The event remains pending threshold and scope review. The 518/approximately 600 returnees are the broader arriving cohort, not turnout. Face of Malawi attributes “only few individuals” to the prison spokesperson; VOA separately reports escapees and arrests, but this worker’s VOA record remains search-only pending coordinator review.
- No continuation is entered. AHL arrears demonstrations may date to Sep–Dec 2020 and a similar minibus action is mentioned in Dec, but exact onset/continuity is unresolved; they remain boundary gaps.

Exclusions
Five leads are excluded from event counting: planned CDEDI actions on Jan 8 and Jan 13 without an actual gathering on those dates; health-worker and water-board strike threats without an executed public assembly; and a Jan 15 minibus association statement with no gathering reported. Their records remain in excluded.json.

Evidence and uncertainty
The Jan 19 march is described as “dozens”; no exact crowd size is reported. The Jan 26 transport event has three contemporary reports, no people-count, tear-gas dispersal in Blantyre/Zomba, and conflicting accounts of representation and the three-per-seat agreement. Malawi24 reports a government–MDCA agreement; The Nation describes an earlier agreement while reporting current enforcement and engagement with MOAM. The organizer/unit and state response remain unresolved.
The Jan 28 FSWA Lilongwe event is reported by The Guardian and Nyasa Times; The Guardian’s “dozens” is insufficient to confirm the 50-person threshold. The District Commissioner explicitly refused the petition. Later movement to the City Council is reported, but its receipt/outcome is unknown. The Guardian separately reports that Attorney General Chikosa Silungwe said the government would look at the issues/respond; this does not establish a meeting or a City Council response. State-response coding remains for coordinator review.
“Dozens” and “only few individuals” are preserved as qualitative source wording, not converted into counts. Retrospective, reposted and linked summaries are recorded with source lineage and are not treated as independent witnesses. No event end date is inferred from the publication date or an announced call-off.

Coverage limits and next research
Fifty-six queries and 40 source records are logged. Searches covered Jan 1–10, 11–20 and 21–31, outlet-specific coverage and Chichewa terms; regional searches added contemporary reports on the Jan 26 transport action. Coverage is strongest for Blantyre/Lilongwe, transport and sex-worker actions; rural/regional radio and Tumbuka/Chiyao coverage remain limited. Several pages were not accessible to this worker after reader errors; ordinary HTTPS attempts failed at DNS resolution, with no observed site denial. The coordinator separately recovered the Zomba and VOA pages through ordinary HTTPS; those recoveries do not change this worker’s recorded access status. Open-web coverage does not reproduce LexisNexis. The official baseline and detailed original Coding Procedure remain pending. No claim is made that no other qualifying event occurred.
