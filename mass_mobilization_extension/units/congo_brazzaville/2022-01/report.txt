Congo Brazzaville — January 2022 first-pass research

Scope and method
This is a first-pass web-search dossier for 1–31 January 2022. It records 28 captured search invocations, with French-first queries plus English variants across weekly periods, labor and education, health, municipal services, police conduct, opposition politics, and Brazzaville, Pointe-Noire and Dolisie leads. Results were search-index extracts and open web material; this did not reproduce the original LexisNexis/newspaper protocol or systematic hemeroteca access. Candidate and exclusion records are provisional; the official baseline and supplementary Coding Procedure remain pending.

January candidate
E001 records reports that the CDEPE began an indefinite volunteer-teacher strike on 31 January over unpaid scholarship arrears, the government agreement and public-service hiring seniority. The reports were published on 1 February. They describe a call addressing eleven departmental bureaus and the coordination base, but provide no actual participation count, workplace-level observation, or public gathering. The 4,660 volunteers assigned under the 2018 protocol are historical staffing context, not turnout. The action is therefore retained as pending_evidence, not treated as a confirmed qualifying MM event. S001, S002 and S008 are variants of one ADIAC reporting origin, not three confirmations.

Earlier-onset continuation
C001 records the Dolisie municipal strike as a continuation observed on 16 January. The Jan. 16 search-result article says municipal offices and the morgue remained locked and that the stoppage had lasted several months. A prefect-chaired agreement included a planned move to minimum service the following week, but the report does not confirm that restart. A July 9, 2021 start is reported for Dolisie among several municipalities; Dolisie-specific continuity from that exact date, turnout and public gathering remain unresolved. It is not a January-onset event.

Other leads and boundaries
The OCDH stories describe advocacy/press activity and alleged police abuse, not a mass protest. La Semaine Africaine reports Mathias Dzon’s Jan. 8 press conference and political call, without a public protest. One SACER article alleges military unrest and a planned palace march on Jan. 5; it relies on unnamed sourcing, has no independent contemporary corroboration in this pass, and describes alleged armed/coup activity rather than a realized public protest.

The later SOPECO sit-in is bounded to a Feb. 7 start by a June report, so it is out of January. The E2C march lead concerns April 8; accounts report a ban and an attempted march with briefly detained organizers, but no large mobilization. These remain boundary records, not January events.

Access and unresolved evidence
S001’s exact PDF URL was opened once after the then-current gate review. The web tool returned “URL is not accessible via this tool,” without HTTP status or denial evidence. The card remains search_only; no alternate route was attempted. After this attempt, the coordinator identified two nested physical historical ledgers absent from the earlier 26-ledger scan. The saved gate receipts remain unchanged and accurately reflect that earlier scope; the two extra ledgers are reported by the coordinator as byte-identical to covered canonical histories. No other body attempt was made.

There is no independent confirmation of the CDEPE strike report, no evidence of a qualifying public assembly or 50 participants, no event-specific state response, and no verified physical end date. Dolisie continuity/restart, local-language coverage, comprehensive local archives and the official baseline remain open. This first pass does not support a claim that there were no other January protests.

Counts: 28 captured searches; 14 source cards; 1 candidate; 6 exclusions; 1 continuation; 11 gaps.
