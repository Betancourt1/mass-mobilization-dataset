Congo Brazzaville — January 2023 first-pass research

Scope
Country: Republic of the Congo (Congo-Brazzaville), provisional ccode 484. Unit: 2023-01. Fixed research cutoff: 2026-10-07, America/Mexico_City. The Mass Mobilization extension remains separate from the integrated protest catalogue; no official observations or approvals were created.

Search coverage
Eight actual grouped web-search invocations were captured with the native header exporter and portable CLI, preserving 200 result headers. The first pass used English and French country/month terms, four weekly windows, local and regional publishers, public-service/education/health/transport sectors, fuel pricing, human-rights reporting, and adjacent-month UMNG follow-up. Date windows were query text, not verified search-engine filters. RFI results were blocked by robots.txt within the grouped search invocation; no retry was made. The search set is a bounded open-web pass and is not exhaustive.

Findings
No January 2023 candidate met the available evidence threshold for an actual public gathering of at least 50 people with a demand to the Republic of the Congo or its policy. This is a search result, not a claim that no event occurred. The opened newspaper excerpt reports a University Marien-Ngouabi intersyndical declaration made public on 24 January, but does not establish an in-person mass meeting, strike, demonstration, attendance, physical event start, or end. The 24 January publication date is not used as an event date. A separate UMNG strike is reported to begin on 18 February; it belongs to adjacent-month follow-up and is not backdated or treated as a January continuation. A customs-office barricade allegation in the same partial issue was described as circulating and denied by the unions; no executed event is documented. The fuel-price increase was announced for 31 January and one search result reported user concern, but no qualifying gathering was verified.

A Goma demonstration on 18 January surfaced repeatedly as a false positive. Goma is in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, so the lead is excluded from this Republic of the Congo unit. Search-only reports include VOA/AFP and TV5MONDE; an AP result is retained but body access was withheld under the inherited AP hold.

Sources and access
Fourteen source cards were saved. One ADIAC newspaper PDF was partially opened after a fresh exact-URL receipt. Read scope: front-page UMNG and customs teasers, publisher imprint, and a partial customs article; the complete issue and UMNG story were not read. The paper’s imprint identifies Les Dépêches de Brazzaville and Le Courrier de Kinshasa as ADIAC publications, so sister-edition copies are not counted as independent corroboration. Eleven cards remain search-only and two remain blocked by inherited AP/Africa-Press holds; no body or syndication-copy requests were made for those held sources. Three durable access receipts are retained in access_receipts/.

Limitations and next review
The original Mass Mobilization codebook/Coding Procedure and current official baseline were not directly available to this worker; baseline_gate remains pending. French and English were used; Lingala and Kituba were not searched. The unit needs contemporaneous evidence to determine whether the 24 January university statement was tied to a qualifying physical gathering, plus local reporting around the 31 January fuel-price change. Gaps preserve these follow-ups, the Amnesty report’s ambiguous “early January” reference, search/press-archive limits, and inherited source holds. No candidates, continuations, or official changes were created.
