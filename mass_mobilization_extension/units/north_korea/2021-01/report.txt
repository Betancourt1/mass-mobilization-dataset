Methods and coverage
Twenty-three query records (Q001–Q023) are saved in search_log.jsonl. The pass combined broad English and Korean protest/demonstration/strike terms with Korean queries on resident complaints, markets, workers, food and labor mobilization; site-restricted DailyNK and KCNA searches; Hyesan/Ryanggang coverage; early-, middle- and late-month week windows; and a December–February boundary search. Most date-limited queries returned unrelated South Korean material, calendars, other dates or general North Korea reporting. Search engines did not reliably honor date operators. Q009–Q011 have null returned_urls because the wrapper truncated one result and the other two calls failed during result capture; they are logged as incomplete, not as empty searches. The work used open-web search, not LexisNexis or systematic newspaper archives. No query result or unsearched archive establishes that no protest occurred.

Two reported rallies
Jan. 15 — A 38 North article (S002, published Jan. 22; web ref turn3694view0) includes a photo caption placing a Pyongyang city rally at Kim Il Sung Square. The caption alone does not identify purpose, organizer or size. Yonhap’s Korean Jan. 17 page (S011, web ref turn3728view0) presents an AI-generated summary and photo caption; both describe pledges to implement Eighth Party Congress decisions, and the caption calls the crowd demonstration “large-scale” without a number. The agent’s page rendering truncated before the original article body. The coordinator separately inspected that body (turn3766view0, lines 76–98), which, relaying KCNA and Rodong Sinmun, confirms supportive speeches and implementation pledges, especially through economic construction and improved living standards. This lead is recorded in excluded.json as a supportive policy rally without a demand directed to the North Korean state. The exclusion does not presume attendance below 50 or voluntary participation.

Jan. 19 — Yonhap’s opened English report (S001, web ref turn3669view0) says joint army-civilian rallies took place in North Pyongan, North Hwanghae, Jagang and South Hamkyong provinces. It attributes its account to KCNA and describes support for Congress decisions and pledges to implement the five-year plan; provincial party committee heads participated. It gives no simultaneous participant counts and identifies no participant demand directed to the state. This lead is likewise recorded as excluded on the reported supportive purpose, not on an assumed sub-50 attendance. The Yonhap report is not independent corroboration of the underlying KCNA account. The Jan. 15 and Jan. 19 leads remain separate; a common campaign or organizer is not established.

Boundary and contextual reporting
A Feb. 15 DailyNK report (S013) was recovered over ordinary HTTPS after a web-reader cache miss. It says a Jan. 29 Hyesan/Samjiyon lockdown was lifted on Feb. 15 after 18 days and reports hardship and residents’ dissatisfaction through an unnamed Yanggang source. It describes no public gathering or protest demand and is not a protest continuation. This distinction preserves contextual hardship reporting without treating it as a mass protest.
A South Korean Ministry of Unification January-monitor catalog lead (S004) lists congress-related rallies, but its underlying report was not read. A Kyunghyang reporter-archive search excerpt (S012) was not verified against a matching direct article; the archive page itself did not surface the reported rally item and is not counted as independent corroboration.

All retrievals were foreground one-shot requests; no server, watcher, browser automation or other persistent resource was started.
The open-source pass has substantial observability limits: no direct KCNA January archive or Ministry-monitor report, no independent account of the two rallies, no reliable attendee estimates, and no systematic domestic press archive. No-event claims would exceed this evidence.
