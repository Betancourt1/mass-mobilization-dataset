Angola, November 2020 — unit research report

Scope and method
This unit covers 1–30 November 2020 in Angola. I executed 22 web searches recorded in search_log.jsonl. English- and Portuguese-language queries covered broad demonstrations, marches and strikes; four week windows; province/city terms; workers, teachers, health, oil and municipal sectors; follow-up after demonstrations; the 11 November demonstrations; and the 28 November Cabinda and Inocêncio de Matos funeral leads. Query text and returned headers/URLs are preserved in the ledger. Five multi-query tool calls returned one shared result list without query-level attribution; that same actual header set is saved with each query row and explicitly marked as shared. The lists therefore do not represent independent per-query result sets. The two single-query calls returned 25 and 18 headers. Captured-header rows total 711 after the required repeated shared-set representation.

8 current-unit body opens; 0 prior-unit-only. Eight opened article cards were read in their rendered views: VOA Portuguese on 21 November; DW on Ndalatando and Luanda on 11 November; RTP on Luanda on 11 November; Euronews on Luanda on 11 November; VOA Portuguese on Cabinda on 28 November; and VOA Portuguese and Novo Jornal on the 28 November funeral procession. These reads are partial rendered-body scopes documented on each source card, not full-page archival captures. Source independence is not assumed: the DW Luanda piece credits both DW reporters and Lusa, while Euronews has a byline distinct from its Lusa/EPA image credit. No body was opened for the held Cabinda article.

Candidate findings
Three candidates meet the stated mass-mobilization threshold on the evidence reviewed: the 11 November Luanda demonstration (DW describes hundreds), the 21 November Luanda gathering (VOA estimates a little over one hundred), and the 28 November funeral procession in Luanda (Novo Jornal describes hundreds and both opened reports characterize it as a protest for justice). The 11 November reports describe police dispersal using tear gas, water jets/cannon and, in Ndalatando, live ammunition; DW and Euronews also report arrests/detentions. Euronews attributes a beating allegation to an activist. Reported deaths on 11 November remain causally unresolved and are not coded as state killings. The 21 November VOA report says police accompanied the gathering and did not suppress it; unreported response categories remain unknown.

The Ndalatando gathering is retained as a pending local alternative because the opened DW report provides no attendance count. Sixteen reported detentions occurred in Kizenga, more than 60 km away, and cannot establish a threshold for Ndalatando. E002 may be subsumed by the multi-location 11 November candidate E003; do not count both without resolving the organization and event relationship. The 28 November funeral procession is a distinct justice protest, not an inferred continuation of the 11 November demonstration. Conflicting claims about the deceased's cause of death are not coded as state responsibility for the later funeral event.

The Cabinda candidate remains unresolved. VOA reports nine activists were arrested while preparing for a planned march and says dozens of activists called it, but supplies no attendance count and does not confirm the march occurred. Correio da Kianda search-result text reports a march and nine detentions; its exact page returned HTTP 403. A fresh exact-URL access check returned hold. The body was not requested again; the source card retains only a limited search-result description. Nine arrests and the organizer estimate are not treated as attendance or proof of a 50-person crowd.

Limits, holds and follow-up
One source was blocked following an observed HTTP 403 and access-check hold; its result is not body-verified. Known publisher/story holds affecting AP, Human Rights Watch, The New Arab, Facebook and L'Orient-Le Jour remain unresolved under the inherited access ledger; this report does not treat those search-result leads as cleared or opened. No workaround was attempted. Other access checks returned manual_review/no exact match and were followed by manual review of publisher and written-origin records before opening; a manual_review result alone was not treated as authorization.

Twenty-two queries included worker/sector searches, but no worker-sector event received a body-read verification in this unit. That is a search-scope gap, not a zero-event finding. The searches and body reads establish evidence from Luanda, Ndalatando and Cabinda, not complete provincial coverage. A full local-press sweep and systematic LexisNexis or major-newspaper archive search were not available/performed. The 11 November death claims and the relation between reported arrests in Kizenga and Ndalatando remain open. The official baseline gate is still pending. These candidates and codes are research proposals pending asynchronous review, not approved official data; counts, unresolved alternatives and gaps should be retained through that review.
