Congo Kinshasa (DRC) — January 2023 first-pass research dossier

This is a provisional country-month research record for the Democratic Republic of the Congo (Congo Kinshasa; provisional ccode 490). It is separate from the integrated Mass Mobilization catalogue. The official baseline, current version, compatibility and use conditions remain pending; no official IDs, approvals, publication or contribution are claimed. The source cutoff for this pass is 2026-10-07.

Search and source scope

Eight actual grouped open-web search invocations produced 243 ordered result headers, all captured with their original headers and local native timestamps. The pass included country and regional discovery, French and English queries, limited Swahili terms, sector leads, and four weekly January date windows. Search-result source cards are kept distinct from article bodies. The source ledger has 37 unique exact-URL cards: 4 with an opened article body, 24 search-only, and 9 not_found after technical retrieval failure. The four accepted current-unit article-body opens are S004 (Anadolu, selected article paragraphs), S040 (Actualite.cd, article excerpt), S043 (Radio Okapi, selected article paragraphs), and S045 (Enquete.cd, selected article paragraphs); 4 current-unit body opens; 0 prior-unit-only source-body consultations. These are bounded excerpts, not full-article claims where noted.

Thirteen exact-URL web.open attempts are recorded: four returned article bodies and nine returned Cache miss without an HTTP status. Three later ordinary HTTPS attempts for ACP (S009), RTVH (S041), and Radio Okapi (S046) failed DNS resolution before HTTP. These results are technical access failures, not publisher denials. All body attempts have their own exact-source receipt and manual scope review. AP (S028) and Africa-Press (S049) remained search-only under inherited publisher holds; no body or alternate copy was requested.

Candidate records

Six physical-action leads are retained. E001 is Radio Okapi’s opened account of a Goma youth sit-in outside the provincial governor’s office on 9 January over construction on state land; turnout and physical end are unknown. E002 preserves an RTPA search-result claim that more than 250 teachers attended a Butembo SYECO/SYNECATH assembly on 11 January; the body, venue, public character, demand target, and attendance claim remain unverified. E003 is Anadolu’s opened report of a Goma anti-EAC demonstration on 18 January; it describes “dozens” of youths, not a count establishing 50, and leaves the direct Congolese-state target unresolved. E004 is the Lubumbashi Ensemble pour la République march: Enquete.cd reports “several thousand” activists on 19 January, and Actualite.cd describes police dispersal over remarks attributed to a serving minister. The qualitative crowd description supports the 50-person threshold without creating numeric bounds. A search-only Radio Okapi card dates a similar action to 20 January, and reports conflict over arrests; the original partisan/electoral exclusion still needs manual review. E005 and E006 preserve search-only reports of separate 21 January anti-EAC marches in Butembo and Beni. Neither has a crowd estimate or opened article body; the Beni snippet’s reported request that the Congolese government equip FARDC remains search-only.

All participant minimum/maximum fields are null: no exact ranges have been invented from qualitative wording. The Lubumbashi event is the sole proposed inclusion based on available evidence; every candidate remains open to human review. No definite exclusions or verified continuations are recorded. The 26 January Goma “ville morte” announcement is retained as an execution gap, not an event: a call for action does not establish that a physical action occurred. Work stoppages and school-closure notices are not treated as public assembly or as evidence of at least 50 participants. Publication dates and last observations are not coded as physical ends; candidate end dates remain unknown.

Coverage limits and follow-up

This was a bounded first pass, not a census. Search results emphasized Goma, Butembo, Beni, and Lubumbashi; Kinshasa, Bukavu, other provinces, rural areas and sectors were not systematically covered. French and English dominated, with only limited Swahili probes. No LexisNexis-equivalent or systematic local newspaper archive was available, and source independence among repeated search-result reports remains uncertain. The 14 gaps record attendance thresholds, state targets, source access and lineage, contested dates/responses, the announced January 26 action, physical end/continuity, local-language/geographic coverage, and the pending official baseline. This work does not establish that no other event occurred.
