Moldova, February 2021 — first-pass open-web research; ready for coordinator review

Scope and method
This is a non-exhaustive first pass for events beginning in Moldova from 1–28 February 2021 under the fixed 2026-10-07 cutoff. The registered rollout was independently re-extracted before handoff: all 28 original search-action records, their exact invocation/query batches, result order and URLs match the durable search_log.jsonl with zero mismatches. The log contains 590 ordered result headers. Some logged actions were batched invocations containing more than one original query; 28 is the number of captured search actions, not a claim of 28 simple query strings or 590 unique/relevant stories.

Searches covered broad and weekly windows, named dates, sectors and publisher domains in Romanian, Russian and English. They exposed local reporting, a Chișinău public-gatherings register, an ANTA record and other news sources. Search result headers/snippets are discovery evidence only. There are 31 source cards and all are search_only. Four physical article-body attempts were made: S001 Diez Romanian returned an Internal Error, S011 Realitatea returned Cache miss, S012 NewsMaker returned Cache miss, and S007 Russian Diez returned Cache miss. None produced usable article text. Separate prerequisite failures and no-request outcomes remain documented individually under access_receipts; they are not additional body reads. The portable CLI reports 0 current-unit body opens and 0 prior-unit-only consultations.

Candidates and exclusions
Four reported gatherings are retained as local candidates: SME owners in Chișinău on 24 February (advance expectation about 400 versus an actual-turnout search result saying only “several tens”), Ocnița CFM wage protests on 22 and 26 February (kept separate; the earlier search result says “several,” while the later result has no count), and the Democracy at Home gathering outside the Constitutional Court on 23 February (no count and uncertain exact state-policy demand). None has evidence establishing at least 50 participants, so none is proposed for inclusion. Each has an unknown physical end date, and violence/state responses remain unknown rather than inferred from silence. No city/worker/vehicle counts are pooled.

Three leads are excluded from the observed-candidate list with reasons retained: the 23 February passenger-route suspension (a service disruption; the reported 1,500 refers to vehicles, not protesters), the 26 February municipal notice for a planned 49-person gathering (no evidence it occurred and the listed count is below threshold), and an Ungheni rail-worker wage ultimatum for which no public gathering or event date is established. No continuation beginning before February is entered. February Ocnița actions are not merged with each other, January's Ocnița action or the possible December-onset Basarabeasca episode without continuity evidence.

Source relationships and limitations
Some returned pages are syndicated/parallel/duplicate variants: Noi.md S003 attributes the SME report to Diez; S007 is a Russian Diez edition; S028 is NewsMaker's Russian edition parallel to its Romanian result; S029 is a second Moldovenii host variant of S010; and Știri.md S020 attributes the transport report to Publika. These are not counted as independent confirmations. Search text varies between PMAN and the Parliament–Government area for the SME event; exact venue remains unresolved. The February 22 rail-worker reports describe track/freight disruption, not a crowd count. A search result says a Presidential Administration secretary would inform Government about the SME complaint, but no policy concession is established.

Open questions include actual attendance and precise venue for the SME gathering; turnout, physical gathering form/site and end for both Ocnița dates; continuity between February 22 and 26 or with January 5; the Constitutional Court gathering's crowd size and state-policy target; all events' physical end and event-specific state response; whether transport operators held an associated public assembly; and whether planned or adjacent labor leads became public gatherings. The first pass does not systematically cover Gagauz-language sources, local/regional newspaper archives, every official source, or a LexisNexis-style newspaper frame. The latest official baseline/universe and full Coding Procedure remain pending. Search coverage is not evidence that no other protest occurred.
