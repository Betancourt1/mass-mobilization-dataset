Bolivia (ccode 145), January 2023 — worker first-pass research report

Scope and coverage
This is a worker-prepared country-month dossier for Bolivia, January 2023. It records 32 actual search invocations and 629 currently captured ordered result headers. Research covered January weekly windows in Spanish and English, national political actions, local city marches, Indigenous-group search terms, the Santa Cruz Camacho-detention campaign, health/labor actions and the Sucre fare dispute. Nineteen partial or incomplete search-log rows were repaired from exact original web.search metadata without replay: the original seven response rows and twelve rows missing native type/domain/ref_id fields. Original log snapshots, recovery manifests, and all nineteen exact response inputs are preserved under capture_revisions. Searches do not establish that no other action occurred.

Candidate actions
The dossier contains 25 event candidates: 6 are proposed as meeting the attendance threshold, 18 remain pending turnout, and 1 need a state-target eligibility decision. These are worker judgments for review, not official inclusions.
The six original worker threshold proposals are the Jan. 2 Santa Cruz women’s march (Infobae’s EFE-attributed report says hundreds), the Jan. 3 Cochabamba civic march (Los Tiempos calls it “massive” without a numeric lower bound), the Jan. 3 Sucre march (reported as hundreds), the Jan. 8 Santa Cruz comparsas/fraternities march (El Deber says more than 3,000), the coordinated Jan. 10 civic protest day (ANF reports hundreds at one La Paz site; the Los Tiempos Tarija passage is credited to Página Siete), and the Jan. 25 national cabildo (ANF and Los Tiempos report thousands in Santa Cruz). E006’s “massive” description is a worker proposal with unresolved numerical support; coordinator review may change its threshold disposition. Los Tiempos’s Jan. 25 byline attributes material to unnamed agencies, so its outlet name does not establish an independent origin from ANF. These sources do not provide a single comparable countrywide attendance count.
The Jan. 3 newspaper issue also reports marches in Santa Cruz, La Paz, Trinidad and Oruro without separate attendance estimates, a separate La Paz pro-government march demanding Camacho be jailed, and a SEDCAM workers’ strike/march over a departmental salary-scale law. The employee count affected by that law is not protest turnout. Local Jan. 2 and Jan. 4 marches, Jan. 5 Tarija/Yacuiba civic actions, the Jan. 6 Sucre fare march, Jan. 11 Tarija MAS-affiliated march, Jan. 22 Santa Cruz women’s march, and Davosan renal-patient hunger strike remain threshold-pending.
The national Jan. 10 action is coded as a coordinated event because civic committees announced a common protest day and ANF reported action across eight cities. Local attendance and reported clashes vary. The Jan. 25 cabildos are also one coordinated national event with highly uneven city attendance. ABI’s under-50 report refers to Cobija at 17:15 and cannot be generalized to the entire event. Its allegation of a later attack on police in Santa Cruz is not independently corroborated.
The Davosan strike began Jan. 4 and was suspended after a Jan. 9 agreement to review/respond to the dialysis contract within 15 days. The highest specific turnout estimate is more than 40; the repeatedly cited 56 are dialysis beneficiaries, not protest participants. The contractual outcome remains unknown.
S015 documents Sucre’s Jan. 30 suspension of the fare increase after municipal/union/Fedjuve meetings, but the causal role of the Jan. 6 march is not established.

Campaign continuity and exclusions
A continuation record documents the Camacho-detention campaign triggered by the Dec. 28 arrest and followed by early-January blockades/vigils and the Jan. 25 cabildo. The first physical gathering date is unknown; the trigger date is not treated as the start of an uninterrupted protest. The Jan. 18 road opening marks an important local blockade phase, not the wider campaign. The precise campaign end remains unknown. One Chonchocoro Red Ponchos gathering was excluded because the opened source gives neither a 50-person count nor a clear demand directed at Bolivian policy rather than a foreign parliamentary visit.
