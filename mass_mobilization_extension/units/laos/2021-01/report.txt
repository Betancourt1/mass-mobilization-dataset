Laos — January 2021 (ccode 812)

Status: first-pass research is open-web researched and pending review. No qualifying January 2021 public assembly was established in this pass. This is not evidence that none occurred. The official baseline and additional Coding Procedure remain unresolved.

Scope and method

The search covered January 2021 in Laos, with December 2020 and February 2021 boundary checks. Thirty-six queries used English, Lao script, and selected Thai, Chinese, and Vietnamese terms. The query set included four January weekly windows; general protest terms; labor, land, transport, education, and health leads; geographic terms; diaspora/location checks; and retrospective RFA, human-rights, government-report, and regional searches. The web tool returned pooled results for four-query calls without a one-to-one query/result mapping or verified result total. The search log preserves the queries and relevant pooled URLs. The open web does not reproduce LexisNexis or systematic local archives, so this is a bounded discovery pass, not exhaustive coverage.

Findings

No January event candidate or pre-January continuation is proposed. A January 29 RFA story reports railway-construction soil damage to farms in Oudomxay and a separate compensation complaint in Sekong. It documents affected households and complaints, not a public gathering, participant count, or a demand made at an assembly (S008; secondary synthesis S009 and S015).

A separate RFA account reports around 100 Guestown-Lao cement-plant workers gathered at the former owner’s home on February 22, 2021. A worker says they had gone to ask for back pay several times in December and January, but that does not establish a public gathering or a state-directed demand on those earlier visits. The documented assembly belongs to February, not January, and concerns an employer wage dispute (S003, S011).

A search result initially suggested a Khammouane worker protest in January, but opened Lao-language RFA and Laotian Times coverage plus a mirrored U.S. report identify that as January 30, 2022; its reported group is about 30 to 35 workers, and the demand concerns unpaid wages from a private employer (S002, S004, S006, S010). The China Project reuses the same source chain (S005); LaoNews could not be reached through local DNS (S007).

Other excluded leads were an October 2020 online hashtag campaign, Thai protests physically in Thailand, and an August 2021 Myanmar-worker protest physically inside Laos. The Thai January protest monitor and iLaw account place their examples in Thailand and describe Thai targets. A Thai PBS article recovered by ordinary HTTPS after a cache miss reports a Jan. 16 action at Bangkok’s Victory Monument directed at Thai state policy; no Laos-directed demand or Lao diaspora participation is established (S012, S019, S020). Participant origin alone does not move an event across borders or months (S013, S014, S017). The brief 2021-in-Laos chronology lists party congress events in January but is not a protest registry and its silence is not evidence of absence (S016).

Records

Candidates: 0. Continuations: 0. Excluded leads retained for review: 7. No response variables were coded because there are no January candidates. Each relevant source card records access state, scope, source lineage, and limits; no full article text was copied.

Gaps and limits

Laos reporting restrictions, incomplete local-language archive access, and uncertain diaspora location mean that a lack of indexed reporting cannot support an absence claim. State Department pages returned forbidden; BHRRC returned HTTP 403; LaoNews failed at local DNS before an HTTP response. No access control was bypassed. Repeated RFA, FIDH, BHRRC, and secondary reports are not counted as independent corroboration where they share the same source chain. The May 2015 local manual is available, but the additional Coding Procedure and authoritative current MM baseline, universe, schema, and license have not been verified. The coordinator must review this research; nothing here is an official dataset update.
