Mali, January 2021 — first-pass research (pending review)

Fifty-two literal queries are logged across thirteen pooled batches. Searches cover weekly windows, local and regional French-language reporting, Bamako and Gao, Kayes, Sikasso, Ségou, Mopti, Tombouctou and Kidal, labor/student/opposition/governance actions, January–February boundaries, and some English-language retrospective sources. No Bambara or other local-language search was completed; Lexis-Nexis/all-news access is unavailable, so coverage is not exhaustive. A January 14 nationwide rally belongs to 2022 and was excluded from this month.

The Jan 20 Bamako anti-French-presence gathering is supported by opened contemporary Anadolu and AFP reporting. Opened Maliweb items are pre-event announcements, not attendance evidence. Reports identify Yéréwolo/Debout sur les Remparts and describe police dispersal with tear gas. Attendance is “dozens” in the opened event reports; a worker-side Reuters result remains search-only in this file, and the domestic-state target is unresolved. Root-retrieved Reuters body evidence is separate and is not retroactively marked as opened here.

A Bamako CDR/M5-RFP caravan occurred Jan 21, demanding release of Ras Bath and other detainees and criticizing transition-authority arrests. HIIK’s opened retrospective report says hundreds; opened Maliweb/Le Pays and search-only Bamada/MaliExpress reporting corroborate the actual caravan. Bamada’s search excerpt reports tear-gas dispersal and no arrests according to a CDR representative. Exact physical closure remains unknown. A separate Jan 22 caravan was planned, but no distinct execution has been verified.

A Jan 27 FORC-G march in Gao against insecurity and seeking state security/peace-process action is reported by ORTM and several local outlets. The governor reportedly received organizers. One Bamada search result says thousands attended, but no report body is opened in this worker’s access record; the candidate remains pending review/evidence. A Jan 28 Mali Tè Kila/Bourse du Travail front-formation lead remains pending: snippets describe opposition to Algiers-agreement implementation but do not establish a public gathering or crowd.

The Jan 3 Bounti strike is excluded as an armed/conflict incident, not a protest. CEN-SNESUP labor action began Jan 5 with an intended five-day strike, but the strike call was lifted Jan 7 after negotiations; no public assembly is documented. Labor, student and public protest evidence are kept distinct.

Nine recorded gaps cover exact crowd counts and the domestic target for Jan 20, direct access and publicness of Mali Tè Kila, incomplete regional/language/archive coverage, the undated HUICOMA sit-in lead, Jan 21 CDR response sourcing and the Jan 22 boundary, Gao source access and turnout, and the CEN-SNESUP strike chronology. No conclusion that no other protests occurred is implied.
