Mauritius, January 2021 — researched by open web; pending coordinator review

Scope and method: The assigned unit is Mauritius (provisional ccode 590), 2021-01. Search is by open web and does not reproduce the manual’s LexisNexis procedure. Forty-eight literal queries have been run in twelve combined requests covering English/French/Mauritian-Creole wording, four weekly windows, Sawmynaden court proceedings, the January 31 meeting, labor/education/health sectors, Mauritius/Rodrigues geography, and targeted attendance follow-up. Each tool response pooled results, so individual query-to-result mapping is unavailable. Exact web-call execution timestamps were not exposed; `executed_at` is null, and pre-call clock reads plus log-record times are documented separately.

Candidate E001 — 12 January: A contemporary L’Express report says sympathizers gathered in support of Simla Kistnen and a crowd massed in the Saint-Louis Cathedral courtyard, chanting for Minister Sawmynaden’s resignation over an alleged fictitious constituency-clerk job. A separate small masked group supporting the minister was moved away from this crowd by police. The Kistnen sympathizers dispersed calmly after the hearing was postponed. No attendance figure or formal organizer is reported. Eligibility remains `pending_threshold`.

Candidate E002 — 31 January: Two Defi Media pages describe the Avengers-led gathering at the La Louise taxi stand in Quatre-Bornes on 31 January. One contemporary page published at 12:48 calls the attendance a “grosse foule” but gives no number; the other page describes activity from 09:30 to 14:00 and currently displays a 2026 date. An L’Express 2 February search excerpt estimates “une dizaine de milliers” (~10,000), and a Mauritius Times interview search excerpt says a participant saw a very large crowd. Those two pages could not be opened, so their qualitative and approximate estimates remain unverified and E002 is `pending_evidence` rather than resolved. The opened Defi Media page reports attendees of varied ages including children, and speeches criticizing state handling of the Kistnen case, seeking truth/justice, calling for the Deputy Commissioner of Police to leave, and challenging the prime minister over Wakashio. Neither opened Defi Media page gives numeric attendance; the L’Express estimate remains search-only. The speaker program is reported as running 09:30–14:00 and named leaders left at about 14:07, but the crowd’s actual physical dispersal is not established; end date is unknown. The other Defi Media page currently displays publication date 6 July 2026 although its search index dates it about 5.7 years ago; that page’s original date/version must be verified.

Candidate E003 — 7 January: A 9 January L’Express search excerpt says hundreds of demonstrators demanded Sawmynaden’s resignation outside New Court House on Thursday 7 January. An 8 January L’Express excerpt also says people came to support Simla Kistnen outside court and that a planned Cathedral Square assembly was moved after police restrictions. A Le Xournal 9 January search excerpt adds that many bystanders were near court, without a count. A contemporaneous ION News excerpt says bystanders gathered near court and one person was arrested, but does not give a number or link the arrest to the crowd. Both pages failed web open and ordinary HTTPS recovery failed local DNS; the specific attendance and demand remain `pending_evidence`. Opened LALIT commentary separately places a crowd in Cathedral Square that day and alleges a disorderly subgroup attacked the minister’s car as it left. LALIT’s attribution of organizing to Bruneau Laurette and its violence claim lack independent corroboration. Keep this candidate distinct from 12 January unless evidence supports continuity. The Police Force notice for 7 January establishes access restrictions, not by itself a qualifying protest.

Separate Wakashio lead: Freedom House’s 2022 Mauritius report says security forces blocked/intimidated peaceful protesters over the spill in January 2021, but gives no date, place, count, or contemporaneous citation. Do not assume it refers to either court-side crowd or to the 31 January meeting.

Boundary/context: A Defimedia search result describes the 29 December 2020 court crowd and says the next hearing was 7 January; that earlier crowd is not evidence of continuity. The August/September 2020 Wakashio protests are prior-month events; the Port Louis opposition march on 13 February 2021 and later Rodrigues demonstrations are post-month events. None is counted here.

Access limits: Several publishers’ web pages returned Cache miss/fetch errors. This worker’s ordinary HTTPS attempts to eight exact public URLs failed local DNS before a publisher response; no HTTP denial was received by this worker. The coordinator separately reports an actual 403 from the L’Express 2 February turnout page, not observed by this worker. Search-only cards remain open leads, not blocked sources.

Final worker handoff: 48 queries logged; 42 URL cards retained, including out-of-month leads; six pages opened and the rest search-only or not directly retrievable through the web tool. One pending-threshold candidate and two pending-evidence candidates are recorded, with no exclusions or continuations and two information gaps. Exact web query execution timestamps were not exposed. Empty exclusions and continuations do not establish absence. Targeted labor/education/health, online-campaign and Rodrigues searches surfaced no January candidate; this does not establish absence. Later Wikipedia results claim a Manan Fakhoo street demonstration before his 20 January death, but no contemporary corroboration was found; the contemporary source lead instead describes the 12 January pro-minister group. Sectoral, regional, language, and archive coverage remains incomplete.
