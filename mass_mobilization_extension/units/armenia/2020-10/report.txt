Armenia — October 2020 first-pass research

Scope and method
This dossier covers Armenia, 2020-10, with the research brief’s evidence cutoff of 2026-10-07 America/Mexico_City. Search response clocks were not exposed. Forty-four actual queries were submitted: 38 retained query responses with 902 captured result headers (551 distinct header URLs), plus six query outputs lost from durable capture. The six lost records preserve exact query prompts and explicitly mark output/header count as unknown, not no results. Two separate batch envelopes record the lost combined responses. Duplicate URLs across searches are not treated as independent sources.

Search coverage included English, Armenian, and Russian; four textual weekly windows across the month in each language; broad protest, demonstration, march, strike, and rally terms; follow-ups about the announced 8 October opposition rally; Armenia government-building and local-news searches; labor, teacher, student, and worker searches; Yerevan and regional-city searches; diaspora/geography distinction; and continuity searches into November. Query date windows do not prove that search-engine date filtering worked. Public web search cannot reproduce the project’s LexisNexis/newspaper procedure. Local archives and search indexing may omit reports; no finding of “no result” means no protest occurred.

Fifteen source cards are retained: eleven opened pages and four search-only records. Seven opened pages document current-unit events/announcements and were read only for relevant metadata and event lines. Four additional opened pages were out-of-period boundary checks, dated 17 and 18 September 2020, 28 April 2020, and 13 November 2019; those date leads were not imported into October coding. Each of the eleven opens was preceded by a fresh exact-URL access check saved in this unit. All checks returned manual_review, which is not permission by itself; exact URLs, publishers, available written-origin relationships, and known holds were manually reviewed before opening. No denial, paywall, login, or anti-bot response was encountered. No full article body is stored; source cards preserve short paraphrases and read scopes. Armenpress is the same publisher for four current-unit article records, so those are not independent confirmation of one another. The search-only CivilNet report may concern the same 16 October UN protest as Shoghakat; that relationship remains unverified. The three-party 8 October announcements use similar language, but exact republication/origin relationships remain unresolved.

Findings
One in-country candidate is retained pending review: a 16 October Yerevan march on Artsakh recognition and ceasefire issues, reported near several foreign embassies and ending at Liberty Square. The report gives no participant count and does not say whether the demand was directed to Armenia’s government or solely to foreign missions. It therefore remains pending_state_target; a 50-person threshold is not established. Its relationship to a separate 16 October UN-office protest is unresolved.

Six documented actions in Armenia were excluded because sources identify foreign embassies or international organizations as the demand target: a UN-office protest on 16 October; actions outside the US Embassy and UN office on 19 October; a German Embassy protest on 21 October; a visit/march to Italian, Ukrainian, and Georgian embassies beginning no later than the report’s second day on 22 October; and an EU Delegation protest on 26 October. Crowd counts remain unknown or below any verified threshold in these sources. One Los Angeles diaspora action is separately excluded by geography; no crowd estimate from it is transferred into Armenia. The report does not treat armed war activity, support for either combatant, or foreign-policy lobbying aimed only at another government as an Armenia event.

Three opposition parties announced a nationwide rally for 8 October and cited poor government performance and a need for a new national government. No located contemporaneous report establishes that the gathering actually occurred or supplies turnout or exact on-site demands. This remains an occurrence gap, not a claim that it was canceled or did not happen.

Search-result date leads about merchants, education protests, and a utility protest were checked against opened reports. They refer to actions outside October (September 2020, April 2020, and November 2019); the September merchants’ action was reported ended. None establishes an October continuation, so the records are retained only as prior-period scope checks. No continuation that began before October was verified. Follow-up searches did not establish a continuation of the candidate into November. End dates, participant violence, and all seven Armenian state-response dimensions remain unknown; silence is not coded as no response. Foreign embassy replies or offers of meetings are not coded as Armenian government accommodation.

Limits and unresolved fields
The supplied May 2015 manual was used. The official project baseline, schema, universe, and exact Armenia code mapping remain unverified; ccode is null. Country-level completeness cannot be claimed. Web search is weaker than LexisNexis and does not guarantee date operators were honored. The six lost query outputs, incomplete local/regional archive coverage, unknown event thresholds, uncertain state target, and unresolved episode boundaries remain explicit gaps. A small Afghanistan 2020-10 source-card sample was consulted only to check dossier field format; no prior event evidence, participant counts, or judgments were imported.
