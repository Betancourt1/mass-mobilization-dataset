Cameroon, February 2021 — first-pass research report

Scope and method
This open-web first pass logged 28 actual English- and French-language searches, including broad and weekly February windows, local/regional and sector queries, month-boundary follow-up, and retrospective searches. The capture ledger preserves all exposed ordered result headers and refs: 535 headers in total; the search tool did not expose a total-results count. Searches surfaced material concerning Buea, Bamenda, Douala, Yaounde and Dschang. Search results were used for discovery only; source cards distinguish search-only leads from article-body reads, and record each body-attempt receipt and its observed outcome. No Africa-Press body or same-story-copy request was made under the saved publisher-wide denial.

Four candidate leads remain unresolved: the 1 February Buea DDR-centre road protest; the 15 February Bamenda DDR march; a nominal 8 February Buea taxi-driver blockade whose February-dated article is near-identical to a July 2020 Cameroon News Agency story; and a Dschang traders’ action whose page-added date does not establish the action date. None has a verified attendance threshold of 50 or physical end. The Buea DDR chronology is also unresolved: contemporary reports place a Buea action on 1 February, while VOA describes one about a week before the 15 February Bamenda march. That may reflect imprecise relative timing or a separate Buea action. The Bamenda centre-resident and aid-recipient counts are not march attendance. Actu Cameroun attributes the 70 Buea aid recipients to Mutations; they are not a protest count. Candidate E003 remains pending evidence and does not establish an independent February occurrence. These cases are preserved separately and are not resolved by search snippets or retrospective leads.

Six exclusions preserve planned-but-unexecuted, support-only, wrong-year or otherwise unverified leads. One continuation record tracks the January SYNES warning stoppage and February university-strike leads; neither blocked report establishes a February public gathering or qualifying attendance. The source ledger contains 25 cards, including opened contemporary reporting, blocked/anti-bot or denial outcomes, and search-only retrospective material. Cameroon Concord has politically loaded framing; available articles do not establish independent sourcing for overlapping Bamenda facts. The staging-domain CrisisWatch result has unverified publisher origin and is not used to resolve chronology.

Access and limits
All body attempts and read scopes are detailed in the source cards and `access_receipts/`. Source cards record the read scope for each body response: several reads covered a full relevant article body, while selected reads covered only the relevant paragraphs; comments and linked stories were generally not read. Cache-miss and DNS failures were retained as technical failures; ordinary public HTTPS fallbacks used fresh receipts. Douala Today returned an anti-bot challenge, Actu Cameroun’s university-strike page returned HTTP 403, and previously recorded exact-URL denials remain attached to the HRLRC and Lebledparle leads; no denied page was bypassed. The Ministry march page remained search-only after Cache/DNS failures. Search-only items are not body evidence.

Search coverage is limited to the open web, English and French queries, and the visible search index. Regional discovery was targeted rather than comprehensive; local-language coverage beyond French and English was not systematic. Licensed LexisNexis access and systematic newspaper archives were unavailable. The official baseline is pending, and absence from this search cannot establish that no other event occurred. Review should resolve candidate eligibility, source lineage, event ownership and missing start/end/attendance evidence before any integration decision.
