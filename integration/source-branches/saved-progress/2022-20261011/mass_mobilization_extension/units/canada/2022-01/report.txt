Canada — January 2022 first-pass research report

Status: researched_open_web_pending_review. This is a provisional research handoff, not an official Mass Mobilization update or coordinator approval.

Scope and method
- Captured 30 open-web search invocations covering weekly January windows, English/French queries, national/provincial convoy context, Montreal health/curfew measures, environmental actions, student coverage and local event leads.
- Retained 50 source cards: 12 opened and 38 search-only at handoff. Opened cards include contemporary local reporting and the Public Inquiry overview; search-only cards retain additional local/official leads and access limits.
- No licensed newspaper archive sweep or systematic census of every province/territory and protest sector was completed. Search metadata are discovery evidence, not a substitute for article-body verification. Agence QMI stories and repeated police-origin accounts are retained as shared provenance, not independent confirmations.

Provisional records
- 13 event candidates: CAN202201-E001–E013. Stronger evidence-backed records include Jan. 5 Montreal, Jan. 22 Montreal, Jan. 23 Waterloo, Jan. 29 Ottawa and Jan. 29 Regina. Jan. 8 Montreal is included provisionally from a search-only several-hundred estimate plus an opened occurrence account. Jan. 1 Montreal remains threshold-pending; Emerson, Vaughan, London-area and Coutts remain pending attendance, unit or onset evidence; Lévis and Edmonton retain state-target uncertainty.
- 11 explicit gaps record baseline/method/coverage limits, attendance thresholds, source-only evidence, event separation, local demand target, source independence and response/physical-end uncertainty.
- 2 continuation records flag Ottawa and Coutts follow-up in February. Jan. 28 Ottawa evidence reports trucks but does not establish human attendance; first direct opened human-crowd report is Jan. 29.
- No candidate is officially approved; all remain provisional for coordinator review.

Important evidence distinctions
- People and vehicles are not combined. Ottawa's Jan. 29 human crowd is not dated to Jan. 28 from truck arrivals. Edmonton's Legislature gathering is kept apart from the Acheson convoy; roadside supporters, Lévis, Emerson, Regina, Ottawa and Coutts are not automatically merged.
- Approximate/qualitative attendance remains attributed; numerical bounds are null unless established. RSVPs, arrest counts, vehicle counts and campaign endpoints are not treated as crowd attendance or physical event endings.
- Most physical ends and event-specific responses remain unknown. Jan. 5 Montreal warnings/dispersal attempt are coded separately from uncertain legal arrest status; Waterloo police presence and rink closure do not establish dispersal.
- S002/S004 body attempts failed before HTTP after cache miss/DNS failure; no publisher denial was observed. S041 was opened only to cover/contents; S049's Ottawa attendance number remains search-only.

Coverage limits and next work
Evidence is concentrated in Quebec pandemic-restriction marches and late-January convoy activity. Jan. 10 Nanaimo, Burnaby and Victoria old-growth actions have search-only occurrence/arrest leads but no turnout; Jan. 17 Vancouver old-growth coverage is an announcement without after-action confirmation. Jan. 24 SFU remains a search-only lead with attendance and state-target uncertainty. Labor, education, Indigenous rights, prices/taxes and events in several regions need better local coverage. Next work should prioritize body verification of captured high-value URLs and the two cross-month continuations, then targeted local/sector reporting; do not treat this pass as a complete country census.
