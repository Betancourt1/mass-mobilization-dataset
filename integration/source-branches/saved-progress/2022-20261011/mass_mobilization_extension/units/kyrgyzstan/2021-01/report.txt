Kyrgyzstan, 2021-01 — provisional open-web research report

Scope and method
The research covers January 1–31, 2021, with boundary checks in December 2020 and February 2021, using 56 targeted English-, Russian- and Kyrgyz-language searches. Queries covered general protest terms, week-by-week windows, sectors/issues, local outlets and regional areas. Relevant sources include local reporting, a human-rights monitor, a regional protest analysis, and the Central Asia Protest Tracker portal. The fixed event cutoff is 2026-10-07 inclusive (America/Mexico_City). The original manual requires a public gathering of at least 50 people making a demand of Kyrgyzstan’s state or policy; campaign promotion alone and private workplace stoppages require separate treatment. Open-web searches do not reproduce the original LexisNexis sampling. Search-only material is retained as discovery evidence and does not verify physical start or event eligibility.

Provisional findings
Eight January candidate/discovery records are retained, two items are provisionally excluded, one pre-January series is recorded as a continuation, and eight gaps remain.

On Jan 5, Kloop reports about 200 people at the Government House/Old Square rally. Presidential candidates urged voters to support parliamentary rule, and Madumarov also explicitly challenged the legality of the constitutional and referendum process and called for legality/new parliamentary elections. It is provisionally proposed_include; the campaign setting remains a manual-review question, not an automatic exclusion.

On Jan 8, an opened Azattyk election-day photo caption attributes about 100 attendees to organizers, while the unopened Kloop result reports around 30. Estimates straddle the threshold, so pending_threshold remains. This gathering advocated a referendum form of government; source detail and the political-rally distinction remain under review.

A Kloop search result describes a Jan 3 Sunday “For Legality” march against the referendum and constitutional changes and reports about 50 people. The article body was not retrieved; physical start remains null and the record is pending_evidence. Search-only Jan 10 reports describe a separate “For Legality” march on referendum day: Azattyk says police estimated about 30 and organizers 50; Kaktus says about 50 but calls it the ninth march while Azattyk calls it the tenth. Neither body was opened. The date is not treated as a campaign meeting alone, but occurrence, count, and unit remain pending. Kloop’s opened Jan 17 article verifies an eleventh Sunday march opposing constitutional change and Sixth Parliament decisions and supporting students seeking lower tuition-contract costs; it gives no attendance count and remains pending_unit. A Jan 31 Kloop search result describes another march and says organizers reported more than 20 people, with no upper bound; it remains a search-only discovery record and does not establish the 50-person threshold.

Search results describe relatives of COVID-19 victims picketing the Health Ministry on Jan 25 and demanding official accountability/removal. The article bodies in this worker record remain search_only, no crowd count is available, and physical start is null pending readable source-body evidence; pending_evidence. On Jan 27, the opened 24.kg report verifies a rally outside Parliament with about 50 participants demanding dissolution, electoral-law changes and state funding for parliamentary campaigns. An AKIpress search result reports around 40 and is blocked by robots.txt, so pending_threshold remains. Neither crowd conflicts nor unreported state responses/endings are collapsed into facts.

One continuation predates January: weekly “For Legality” / anti-constitutional marches in central Bishkek. ACLED/Oxus places the start in mid-October 2020 and says the series ended when amendments were adopted on Jan 10. Kloop’s opened Jan 17 report confirms a later weekly march; Jan 31 search text and a March 1 Media.kg result also describe continued Sunday activity. The event-versus-campaign unit and physical end are unresolved. Dates and attendance from one Sunday are not transferred to another, and no record is automatically merged with this continuation.

Two items were provisionally excluded. An opened 24.kg report describes “several people” protesting a proposed minibus fare increase at City Hall on Jan 25, with no evidence of 50. An opened Kloop report describes a Jan 18–19 driver work stoppage over fares but no public assembly of 50 people; vehicles or unassembled workers are not counted as crowd participants.

Access and coverage limits
Twenty-seven source cards are retained. Query results were returned in combined batches without per-query rankings or total counts, so the log records visible relevant URLs and explicitly does not claim exhaustive inventories. Several pages produced cache misses; a later same-public-URL HTTPS retry for the Jan 3 Kloop article failed local DNS. A site-targeted search explicitly reported an AKIpress robots.txt block, which was respected. The CAPT portal opened, but its event-level download returned an unsupported content type and remains uninspected. Regional and language coverage is selective; the searches produced no new verified event outside Bishkek, which is not evidence that none occurred. “Not found” is not “did not occur.” The full Coding Procedure and official baseline/access status remain pending.

Review priorities
Readable contemporaneous bodies and crowd evidence are still needed for Jan 3, Jan 8, Jan 10, Jan 25 and Jan 31; Jan 17 needs an attendance estimate and unit ruling; Jan 27’s around-40/around-50 conflict needs review. Apply the event/episode rules to the weekly series without merging dates automatically. Search gaps, source access, unreported state response and physical end remain open. The official baseline and full additional Coding Procedure are not established.
