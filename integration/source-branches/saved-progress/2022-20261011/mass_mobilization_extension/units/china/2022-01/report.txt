China January 2022 first-pass research

Status: open-web first pass documented; all candidate evidence remains search-only and coordinator review is pending.

Scope and method
A total of 23 web-search invocations were captured immediately in search_log.jsonl. Queries covered English and Chinese-language country/month protest, demonstration, gathering and strike terms; weekly/date-specific windows; labor, workers, residents, lockdown/food access, housing/owners, students/teachers, land/environment and other local-action terms; and targeted follow-up for found leads. Search headers and native result references were preserved. Search snippets were used only to identify leads and are not treated as opened articles or independent confirmations. The source ledger now has 28 URL cards. Nine relevant context/boundary cards were added after the shared ledger window closed, and seven existing cards received additional captured discovery references.

Seven provisional candidate leads are preserved in events.json: Tianjin food/lockdown gathering (E001); Shenzhen neighborhood lockdown gathering (E002); Xi’an Huacheng International gathering (E003); a separately recorded possible Xi’an police-station gathering pending episode-boundary review (E004); Zhuhai wage-action report (E005); Liuzhou construction-worker wage-action report (E006); and a disputed Chongqing Jinke headquarters wage-claim gathering (E007). No candidate is proposed as approved. The three resident/lockdown leads lack verified attendance thresholds except that one Xi’an snippet reports nearly 100 owners; the Chongqing report says more than 100, but both remain snippet-only. The labor reports do not establish public attendance or an own-state target. Candidate distinctions are intentionally retained; no multi-city campaign or same-day actions were automatically merged.

Eight leads are excluded in excluded.json: a one-person Shenzhen banner incident; Jinan teachers’ online complaints; a dumpling-worker livestream complaint; Canon Zhuhai severance negotiations without a documented public gathering; two pre-January individual incidents; Xi’an online-post/complaint leads without a documented qualifying public gathering; and early-2023 wage gatherings listed in a later retrospective. No confirmed January continuation of an earlier protest was identified, so continuations.json is empty; the lockdown’s start in December is not itself coded as a protest continuation.

Access, origin and uncertainty
No publisher article body was read. For S001, exact-URL web.open timed out at tool level, and one same-exact-URL ordinary HTTPS fallback failed local DNS before HTTP. For S005, web.open returned a tool-side Cache miss, and same-exact-URL HTTPS also failed DNS before HTTP. These observations are not publisher denials: no publisher HTTP response, denial, login, paywall, challenge or body was observed. All linked sources remain search_only. Search results suggest reports may share videos, local social posts or republished stories; their origin relationships are unresolved and duplicate coverage is not counted as independent corroboration. Reported detentions and alleged violence are left unknown or attributed only where a snippet explicitly says so; no silence is coded as absence.

Coverage and follow-up
Captured searches included both English and Chinese queries and examined timing across the month, plus labor, lockdown/food, residential/owner and boundary leads. They do not reproduce LexisNexis or a systematic local-press archive. Search indexing and censorship, inaccessible/deleted local posts, the snippet-only evidence, uncertain episode boundaries, state-target requirements for labor leads, response attribution, December continuity, and the pending official baseline are recorded in gaps.json. “Not located in this open-web pass” is not interpreted as “did not occur.”
