Botswana — January 2022 first-pass research

Status: researched_open_web_pending_review. This is a provisional country-month dossier, not an official update or a finding that the month contained no protest. The assigned code 571 and current baseline remain provisional.

Method and coverage

Captured 14 actual web.search invocations immediately after each call and retained 17 relevant source cards: 4 opened, 3 blocked or paywalled, and 10 search-only. Searches covered the four January week windows; protest, demonstration, march, strike, and local-language terms; Gaborone, Francistown, Maun, Palapye and other regional leads; labor, students, living costs and transport, land, electoral process, rights and press-freedom policy; and follow-up across December 2021 and February 2022. Local Botswana outlets and official/rights sources were queried. Setswana terms boipelaetso and ditshupetso were attempted, with sparse or unrelated results.

The open web does not reproduce LexisNexis or systematic newspaper archives. These searches do not establish exhaustive coverage; an empty query result is not evidence that no event occurred.

January candidate

One unresolved candidate is retained as pending evidence: botswana_2022-01_E001, the Joint Tertiary SRC living-allowance petition submission. The Botswana Gazette’s 19 January issue, printed p.3, reports a recent petition seeking a 35% increase and says students were “not rowdy when they submitted the petition,” suggesting a possible in-person group. The report gives no exact submission date, venue, or attendee count. The 17 February Sunday Standard follow-up (article lines 108–121) says the Joint Tertiary SRC represented SRCs from 18 tertiary schools and made the demand at the start of 2022; 18 schools is not a crowd count. It says the “biggest demonstration” was still looming on 17 February, so that future demonstration is not assigned to January. The candidate remains pending whether the January petition involved a public gathering and whether 50 or more people attended. Its state allowance demand is recorded separately from the private Mupane wage dispute. Seven state-response values remain unknown.

Excluded and boundary leads

botswana_2022-01_X001 records Mupane Gold Mine’s private salary/bonus dispute separately. The 111 figure in Mupane reports refers to employees or union members represented by BMWU, not protest attendance. The 31 January Sunday Standard report contains a sentence saying workers initiated a strike, but the union secretary says they were preparing strike rules and returned to mediation to avert the looming strike. The 17 December Mmegi page’s accessible excerpt says a cooling-off period began 2 December and no strike could take place during it; the page then requires sign-in/unlimited access and was not pursued further. Even if work stoppage occurred, the sources do not establish a public gathering or demand on Botswana state policy; the private employer issue is excluded.

botswana_2022-01_X002 records the UDC’s supplementary voter-registration demand as a letter/legal threat to the IEC, read in the Gazette’s 19 January issue, printed p.5. No public gathering or attendance is described. Search-only leads also included BCP’s proposed parliamentary urgent motion over constitutional review, criticism of the controlled-investigations bill, a LEGABIBO meeting with the President, and cross-border advocacy related to the Nchindo inquest; available leads did not establish a qualifying public gathering.

Transport boundary review found a BOPA report carried by DailyNews stating combi/taxi operators downed tools on 22 November 2021 at/around Gaborone Bus Rank. It says talks with transport-ministry officials produced an amicable solution on 23 November and operators were urged back to work. “Scores” refers to stranded commuters; operator attendance is not stated. A 15 December Gazette result announced possible mobilization for another strike before the end of January, but the exact PDF URL returned an actual 403. No January occurrence/onset report was found in the targeted follow-up searches. The November action is not moved into January.
