Bolivia — January 2022 research handoff

Status: researched_open_web_pending_review. This dossier records a first-pass open-web search, not an exhaustive event inventory or official Mass Mobilization coding approval. The official baseline/codebook gate remains pending.

Coverage and methods
- 17 captured search invocations; 61 source cards; 13 article/statement bodies read; 13 event candidates; 2 exclusions; 0 verified cross-month continuations; 13 open gaps.
- Searches were organized around weekly/date slices and regional and sector terms, predominantly in Spanish. Search and source records retain the executed-query IDs and result-order provenance. Open-web retrieval does not reproduce LexisNexis; Oruro, several departments and Indigenous-language reporting remain thinly covered.
- Each body-read source has an access receipt and logged access history. Search-only leads remain marked as such; technical Cache/DNS failures were not treated as observed denials and were not repeatedly retried.

Recorded candidates
- Two proposed includes: the 10 Jan El Alto–La Paz march, described as “massive” by Los Tiempos without a numeric count; and the 31 Jan El Alto–La Paz women’s-rights march, for which EFE reports hundreds at that route only.
- Eight pending-threshold candidates include the 10 Jan Cochabamba march; K’ara K’ara/Petrolera blockade (17 Jan onset inferred from ERBOL’s “second day” on 18 Jan and “third day” on 19 Jan); several distinct Jan18 regional blockades; the 31 Jan Cochabamba transport-sector counter-blockades; and the separate Jan31 Potosí and Santa Cruz women’s actions.
- Three pending-evidence leads remain: the El Alto Naciones Unidas blockade with conflicting body-read accounts, a Jan11 campesino march whose body was not read, and a Jan4 Santa Cruz anti-certificate protest lead whose body was not read.
- The Jan18 SIRMES La Paz strike was announced but then suspended before execution and is excluded as an unexecuted plan. A Jan3 threat related to Marco Pumari produced no verified public gathering and is also excluded.

Evidence boundaries and unresolved fields
- The 13 Jan nationwide strike/blockade announcement is not treated as proof of execution; local Jan17–19 blockades are separately dated.
- K’ara K’ara’s Jan17 start is an attributed day-count inference. No body-read evidence establishes its durable physical end or February continuation. Police use of chemical agents/attempts to clear the road are reported, but clearing assembled persons is not established for coding crowd dispersal. The vaccine-card/PCR suspension is campaign-level policy context, not a verified site-specific accommodation.
- No demonstrator count is inferred from hundreds of affected vehicles/road users. The Jan31 EFE “hundreds” estimate is limited to the El Alto–La Paz march; it does not apply to Potosí or Santa Cruz.
- Los Tiempos’ Jan31 page is ANF-origin; Swissinfo’s report is EFE-origin. EJU’s Jan18 and Jan20 items explicitly republish Página Siete content. These origins remain distinct; republications are not independent confirmations.
- Femicides reported in connection with the Jan31 march are motivations, not killings in response to the march. No violence, state response, attendance or end date is transferred across cities or separate blockade sites without evidence.
- Search-only access gaps, source independence, language coverage, site-specific attendance, state responses, and precise episode boundaries remain open as itemized in gaps.json. Unknowns are retained; no absence claim is made for the remainder of the month.
