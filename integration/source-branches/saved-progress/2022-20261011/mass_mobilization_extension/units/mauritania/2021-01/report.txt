Mauritania — January 2021

Status: researched_open_web_pending_review. The first-pass open-web research is complete for this unit; independent review is pending. The limitations and unresolved leads are recorded below.

Scope and method. Provisional country code 435; event window 1–31 January 2021. I ran 92 literal open-web searches in Arabic, French, and English, aimed at general event terms and the Amel-programme worker issue. Search results were pooled within batches, and the tool did not expose query-specific totals. The project’s open-web method cannot reproduce LexisNexis. Weekly, sectoral, and regional coverage—including Nouadhibou and other wilayas—remains incomplete.

Current January lead. Search-only Alakhbar result S002 reports a 20 January sit-in by Amel-programme workers before the TAAZOUR offices in Nouakchott, seeking employment-status resolution and related action by national authorities. The page did not open: the web reader returned Cache miss and an ordinary HTTPS request failed locally at DNS resolution before reaching the site. The result gives no gathering size, so candidate E001 remains pending_evidence and below no established threshold conclusion. Do not infer attendance from workforce totals. Protester violence, all seven state-response fields, and end date are unknown.

Boundary lead. Opened French report S003 was published 4 January and describes recurrent sit-ins by the same broad workforce, including a “latest sit-in” before the presidency. It does not provide that gathering’s occurrence date; publication date is not substituted for it. Its phrase referring to several hundred people is the population for whom a right was claimed, not a count of participants. The relationship between this undated sit-in and the 20 January sit-in remains unresolved.

Access and corroboration. S001, an opened Alakhbar report of several separate sit-ins on 3 February, is outside the assigned month, gives no attendance count, and does not prove continuation from January. S004 is an unopened union-federation search result with date unclear. S005 is a search-only repost of a 1 February union statement; workforce figures in it do not establish protest turnout. No candidate is approved.

Additional labor leads: a 13 January search result reports that some public-school contract teachers refused to resume classes after the COVID-related closure. It does not establish a public gathering or attendance, so it remains a follow-up lead and is not coded as an event. A separate search-only Politicien result attributes a presidential-palace sit-in to the General Union of Amal Program Workers but provides no visible page date; it is not assigned to January or merged with E001.

A targeted four-query search for the Jan 20 workers’ sit-in and its attendance repeated existing reports and did not reveal a crowd estimate.

A separate 15–21 January sweep for regional, education, labor, women’s, opposition, and rights-related activity surfaced no new in-month gathering source; visible related results were outside the assigned month.

Rural Brakna candidate E002: an opened FPC report of a 23 March press conference attributes to a Feralla spokesperson the claim that a sit-in was organized in the Koylal plain on 22 January to recover ancestral lands amid state irrigation works. It gives no attendance estimate. Another FPC account says farmers threatened a sit-in, and a secondary chronology dates that threat to 25 January; whether these are the same action or different stages remains unresolved. A separate Initiative News result documents a 22 February sit-in with an unquantified “impressive crowd,” which cannot establish January attendance. An IRA visit dated 30 January and the World Bank’s late-February statement are not treated as the sit-in or as event-specific state responses. E002 remains pending evidence and the 50-person threshold is unknown.

A separate 1 January Al-Hurriya search result reports gold prospectors protesting Maaden Mauritania’s closure of mining areas despite paid permits, opposing the decision and calling for authorities to intervene. It says hundreds of prospectors and union representatives came to the area rejecting the closure and that protesters called for authorities to intervene; whether this describes simultaneous protest participants is not fully clear. The visible date is publication date, not an explicit occurrence date. An official Maaden agency-profile result says the organization was created in 2020 as a public industrial and commercial institution under technical supervision of the Ministry of Mines and Industry; the official Arabic profile was opened and confirms public-institution status, technical ministerial supervision and a May 2020 establishment decree (S019). It does not resolve the protest’s occurrence date or attendance. The web reader returned Cache miss; an ordinary same-URL HTTPS retry failed before connecting because local DNS resolution failed. E003 is retained in the events file with an unknown start date and only provisional January-unit ownership; the published 1 January date is not used as an event date, and actual onset may belong to an adjacent month. Repeated exact-phrase searches returned the same report, not independent corroboration. Later 22 February search-only reports from Al-Khabar and Al-Hadath concern a separate Chami tax/ore-transport fee dispute and roadblock; they do not establish January date or turnout.

Separate Arabic/French/English searches targeting 22–31 January and dated Cridem/Alakhbar results returned mostly wrong-year or out-of-month hits; the visible cluster supplied no new in-month 2021 gathering source. Searches of local outlets and follow-up queries for the 26 January teacher sit-ins repeated the announcement and found March or foreign-country actions, but no execution report. These searches do not establish that no event occurred.

An opened 10 January Zahra Info article (S020) reproduces a Sahel TV workers’ statement about a work stoppage beginning 1 January over six months of wage arrears. It lists 17 workers who stopped on 1 January plus four who had stopped earlier, but these are workers in the stoppage, not a public crowd. No public assembly or state-policy demand is established, so the lead is documented in the exclusions file rather than coded as an event.

An opened education-union announcement published by Watanicom on 24 January (S021) announced a press conference and planned sit-ins for 26 January at wilaya/department buildings during teacher evaluation. This is a plan, not proof of a gathering; attendance and actual locations remain unknown. G010 records the follow-up.

An RFI article republished by Le Journal Africa (S022) is dated 18 January and says that 300 teachers attended a Sunday press conference transformed into a meeting, implying 17 January. This is candidate E004, pending review of the source-only result and whether the meeting venue was a qualifying public assembly. The report separately says a Ministry sit-in was planned for Monday; its execution and attendance are unknown and it is not merged with the Sunday meeting. The reported 3,600 refers to teachers who passed the competition/workforce, not attendees. An opened Jan1 letter to the President (S027) confirms the public-service integration demand and mentions an affected worker cohort of more than 4,000, also not meeting attendance. RFI was blocked by robots.txt and the repost reader returned Cache miss. A Jan8 report says service-provider teachers had already begun protesting for regularization, but date, form and size are unknown; a Jan22 Ministry statement is not tied to any particular gathering. G011–G013 track these gaps. A Jan8 Alakhbar interview says the campaign began on 21 December 2020 and a Jan23 union statement reports that a strike was suspended; neither establishes a public-assembly continuation into January, so continuations.json remains empty.

A final Arabic/French/English search batch for opposition, human-rights, and women-led activity returned September 2021 and March 2021 reports but no new January 2021 in-scope gathering. These out-of-window results do not establish absence.

Current counts: 92 searches, 27 source cards (8 opened, 19 search_only), 4 candidates, 1 exclusion, 0 documented continuations, and 13 gaps. Weekly, sectoral, and regional coverage remains incomplete. The records do not show that no other protests occurred.
