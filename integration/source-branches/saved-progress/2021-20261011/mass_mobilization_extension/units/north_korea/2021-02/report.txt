North Korea — February 2021

Research status: researched_open_web_pending_review. No qualifying February 2021 protest event was established in the accessible sources; this is not a claim that no protest occurred.

Method and coverage

Twelve actual open-web search actions were captured with 46 query expressions and 249 ordered result headers. Searches used English and Korean, with Japanese and Chinese discovery queries, and covered protest/demonstration/strike terms, residents and workers, Hyesan lockdown complaints, market restrictions, forced mobilization, relevant South Korean/international publishers, rights reporting, and January–March boundaries. Search responses and exact header arrays are retained in search_log.jsonl. Sixteen source cards preserve relevant opened and search-only URLs.

Ten source cards have documented body reads: S001–S005, S008–S009, S011–S012 and S016. S009 is limited to page one of a two-page Korean report. S016 is a partial read of a long testimony-index homepage. Six cards remain search-only: S006, S007, S010, S013, S014 and S015. Publisher pages and records may share anonymous interview or local-source material; independence is not inferred from different outlet names.

Findings

The captured sources describe a January 29 Hyesan/Samjiyon lockdown, its reported end after about 18 days, food and market hardship, inspections, private complaints, and a state-organized anniversary rehearsal campaign. The opened accounts do not describe a February public protest or collective public demand. RFA’s March 1 report offers later reporting about early-February enforcement and complaints, not a described public action. The compulsory rehearsal campaign and a historical hunger-strike account are retained as excluded leads with reasons.

S016’s body ties the Hyesan hunger strike to a November 2–20, 2020 lockdown, outside this unit. The account provides no turnout, public venue, organizer or specific public demand; the profile’s 2019 escape year conflicts with the 2020 chronology. Its search-preview association with February 2021 is not corroborated by the passage read. No February event or cross-month protest continuation is coded.

Coverage limits and review

North Korea’s domestic press and many local records are not openly accessible. Open-web searching is not LexisNexis or a systematic newspaper archive, and the six search-only sources, S009’s second page, original interview records and linked materials were not reviewed. Anonymous accounts, sparse reporting and uncertain source independence leave a substantial possibility of unobserved events. The result means no qualifying episode was established by this documented research, not that no protest occurred.

The local May 2015 Clark–Regan manual was consulted for the public-gathering, 50-person and state-directed-demand criteria. The latest official baseline and additional Coding Procedure remain pending; this dossier is not an official update or approval.
