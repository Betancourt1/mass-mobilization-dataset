### Khabarovsk Furgal-support sequence

An opened MK Khabarovsk report dated Jan. 9 describes a traditional Saturday rally at Lenin Square and attributes an estimate of more than 100 attendees to eyewitnesses. The report identifies a “Free Furgal” demand and says weekly actions began after his July 2020 arrest. A contemporaneous Vedomosti report and an Interfax report establish an earlier July 11 rally, its state-directed demands, and attributed turnout estimates; those July counts are not assigned to January. The January gathering is E004, pending unit/episode review, and C001 separately records the older-onset weekly-action series without claiming continuous occupation. The MK photo caption says “today, 9 December” despite the page/body context indicating Jan. 9; G011 preserves this conflict. RBC and NEWSru Jan. 2 page opens produced tool-level Internal Error/Cache miss responses; no body was read and no site denial was observed.

The Jan. 9 source clears the attendance threshold at one named site, but does not establish state responses. The exact relation between a weekly action series and the event unit remains uncertain; the later 2020 campaign is not treated as automatic evidence of a January onset or January turnout.

### Bounded Jan. 23 fatality evidence

The Guardian’s Jan. 23 report says no deaths had been reported at the time of publication. This supports only the bounded no_explicit coding in E001; it is not a claim that deaths were permanently ruled out or independently verified.


The Jan. 9 MK report says the action lasted briefly; because it gives neither a precise end time nor an explicit departure/dissolution, E004 retains a null end date while preserving that qualitative duration. A Jan. 8–14 search surfaced a separate Jan. 10 ambulance-worker threat, but the opened Riddle report dates it to 2024, so it is not included for Jan. 2021. A third-party Wikipedia mirror snippet points to possible Jan. 18–19 and Jan. 30 Khabarovsk entries; the mirror page produced a tool-level Internal Error, and these are recorded only as follow-up leads (G012).


### Khabarovsk within the Jan. 23 action

Interfax’s contemporaneous report describes one Jan. 23 Khabarovsk action supporting both Navalny and Furgal. It attributes about 250 participants to the city administration and 200–300 to DVhab; these same-site estimates clear the threshold but are not summed. The report says several participants were detained and attributes an estimate of at least ten taken to DVhab. A 14:48 local update says the rally had nearly subsided, which does not prove complete departure and does not set the end of the national wave. The same physical action is included under E001 rather than duplicated as a separate Furgal event. The linked city-administration page and DVhab report returned tool-level Cache miss/502 and were not directly inspected.

The Jan. 2 Khabarovsk reports conflict: NEWSru’s headline/partial page says several dozen people gathered despite an official cessation claim, while a Vedomosti result reports the municipality’s claim. NEWSru returned HTTP 200 but only the title and start of the lead were captured; Vedomosti’s exact result URL normalized and returned 404, while RBC returned 401. The Jan. 30 RFE/RL article was later recovered in full through one ordinary HTTPS request to its exact public URL (HTTP 200; 90,580 bytes; exit 0; SHA-256 recorded in S085). The conflict leaves E006 pending_threshold; detention counts do not establish turnout. DW also describes a separate Jan. 31 Khabarovsk group of 70–80 people and a Feb. 6 single picket; the Jan. 31 local action’s relation to the national Navalny call is unresolved (G014), and the Feb. 6 picket is below threshold/outside this unit. OVD-Info’s Jan. 30 exact URL remains blocked with HTTP 403. The linked Khabarovsk city and DVhab Jan. 23 sources could not be directly read (timeout and title-shell-only response); Interfax’s attributions are preserved. No denied URL was retried or bypassed.
