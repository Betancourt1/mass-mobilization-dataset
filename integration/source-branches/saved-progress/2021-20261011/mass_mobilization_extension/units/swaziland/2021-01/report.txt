Swaziland / Eswatini, January 2021 — researched, open-web pending review

I ran 25 separate open-web searches through the fixed cutoff of 7 October 2026 (inclusive, America/Mexico_City). Coverage included all four January weekly windows; both the inherited Swaziland and current Eswatini names; general protest, demonstration, march and strike terms; two siSwati-term probes (umbhikisho and kuteleka); local news archives; labor/teachers, students, political-democracy, land/farming and economic terms; named towns and regions; official-government and regional South African sources; and December 2020/February 2021 continuity. One targeted Observer-domain search returned zero results. Search outputs often ignored date terms or returned the well-documented May–October 2021 protest wave. Open-web searching does not reproduce systematic newspaper-archive access, so the search is not exhaustive.

No January 2021 event is supported by the captured evidence. events.json, excluded.json and continuations.json remain empty; no candidate, exclusion or prior-onset continuation was established. Because no occurrence is substantiated, I did not create an event object or assign state-response values. I do not claim that no qualifying gathering occurred.

The main local leads were checked carefully. An opened January 30 profile reports that PUDEMO members and leaders attended Mario Masuku’s funeral, but gives no exact funeral date, attendance size, demonstration action or demand directed to the Eswatini state. A separate opened January 27 notice says Labour Minister Makhosi Vilakati’s state funeral was scheduled for January 28 and offered virtual viewing; the notice reports no protest action, grievance or turnout. Neither lead meets the evidence needed for a candidate. An opened local report describes a tertiary-student petition march to the Prime Minister’s offices on 16 November 2020, with education and police-related demands; it is outside January, gives no attendance count and does not report physical continuity into January.

Searches and chronology sources place the later student/teacher and pro-democracy protest wave in May/June 2021 or later. Those events are outside this unit and were not moved into January. January reports of deaths or Cyclone Eloise were not treated as gatherings.

The 11 unique source cards contain four opened pages and seven search-only pages. The opened pages are the eSwatini Protests timeline (read as later-wave context only), the Swati Newsweek profile, the Independent News Eswatini state-funeral notice, and the November 2020 student report. For the Swati Newsweek page, web.open first returned a cache miss. An ordinary HTTPS attempt in the default sandbox then failed at DNS resolution (exit 1; no HTTP response). The same exact public URL was fetched through the permitted request: HTTP 200, final URL unchanged, 193,744 bytes read, exit 0. The HTML was read in memory for relevant facts; no article copy was saved. The other three pages were opened normally by the web tool, which did not expose HTTP status. No actual 403, 404, login, paywall or anti-bot denial was encountered.

Six gaps remain: open-web versus systematic archive coverage, unresolved official baseline and Coding Procedure, unknown funeral date/attendance/action for the Mario Masuku lead, lack of systematic local newspaper backfile access, limited siSwati discovery, and incomplete adjacent-month episode coverage. Baseline compatibility remains pending. No official IDs or event-response codes were assigned.

Two persistence issues were corrected without rerunning searches. The Q012 persistence command returned exit 0 but wrote a literal backslash-n delimiter in the query row and status/report text; the files were repaired from the stored tool output with a repair command that returned exit 0. For Q018, the query row was persisted first; the initial source-card write then stopped with NameError (exec exit 1). I recovered the card from the stored query log and opened-page result, repaired it with a command that returned exit 0, and did not rerun Q018.
