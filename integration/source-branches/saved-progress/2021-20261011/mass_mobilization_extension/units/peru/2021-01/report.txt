Peru | January 2021 | open-web research first pass

Scope and status
This unit covers physical protest episodes with onset from 2021-01-01 through 2021-01-31, inclusive. The research cutoff is 2026-10-07 inclusive in America/Mexico_City. Peru's country code 135 is provisional. The official baseline, current event universe, and schema compatibility remain pending coordinator review; no official IDs or approvals are claimed. This is a documented first pass, not an exhaustive census or a finding that unlisted protests did not occur.

Coverage and source access
Fifteen one-query-per-call web searches were recorded, with 374 returned search-result records and 73 source cards: 27 opened and 46 search-only. Full web-tool text is not redistributed; captured result headers, URLs, and refs remain in the query log. Searches included broad Spanish discovery, weekly windows for Jan 1–7, Jan 8–14, Jan 15–21, and Jan 22–31, targeted follow-up on health, quarantine, accountability, transport, gas prices, Cusco and Puno, and exploratory Quechua/Aymara-related keywords. The primary structured lead was Defensoría del Pueblo Report 203, which lists 135 collective protest actions for January. Its action table on printed pages 106–116 was screened row by row for date, action, actor, place, and demand. The table does not give participant counts or physical endings.

This coverage has material limits. Q005 returned 31 records, but seven records have unrecoverable title/URL metadata in the original capture; their nulls and the limitation are retained in Q005 and G010. The Puno/Juliaca query combined Spanish with a few Quechua/Aymara-associated terms, but results drifted mainly to later years; no fluent-language review or systematic Indigenous-language archive search was completed. La República and Diario EP leads (S070–S072) returned cache misses; ordinary HTTPS requests to their exact URLs failed at DNS resolution before connecting. No bypass was attempted, and those items remain search-only. Open-web search cannot reproduce the historical LexisNexis corpus or its expansion method. Relevant local print, broadcast, radio, union, and community archives may be missing.

Candidate records
Eleven event candidates are preserved in events.json. Two are proposed inclusions and nine remain pending the attendance threshold; none is finally approved.

- E001: Jan 30 Lima anti-quarantine march, pending threshold. AS Peru reports more than 1,000; El Comercio describes “decenas” (dozens), so no event-level lower bound is assigned. A police cordon blocking passage toward the Palace is reported, but no source says the assembly was dispersed. Protester violence is coded yes based on El Comercio's event-specific account of sticks and stones; other state-response dimensions are unknown.
- E002: Jan 31 Plaza San Martín quarantine-policy gathering, pending threshold. Diario Correo explicitly dates a gathering to Jan 31 and describes dozens; AS Peru says up to 100. Neither account establishes a minimum of 50. The relation to Jan 27 and Jan 30 gatherings remains unresolved; a shared campaign key is tentative and does not merge them.
- E003: Jan 27 Lima march against announced quarantine measures, listed in Report 203 row 110. Turnout, physical end, and relationship to later marches remain unverified; pending threshold.
- E004: Jan 13 Federación Médica Peruana plantons, proposed inclusion. RPP reports 200 health professionals at the Lima Minsa site, which supports the threshold there. Report 203 rows 31–34 list contemporaneous actions in Lima, Trujillo, Iquitos, and Tacna; the Lima count is not a national total, and same-day multi-site grouping remains tentative. Physical end is unknown.
- E005: Jan 28 FENUTSSA plantón, Report 203 row 117. No attendance count or independent contemporary body was found; pending threshold.
- E006: Jan 28 FEDEMINSAP plantons, Report 203 rows 119–122, with four reported bases. Attendance at the sites is unreported; the sites are tentatively grouped and no turnout is summed; pending threshold.
- E007: Jan 9 Lima march by #14N victims and supporters demanding accountability for police violence, proposed inclusion. El País reports “cientos” (hundreds), supporting the threshold. Earlier deaths and shootings are antecedent grievances, not Jan 9 state responses.
- E008: Jan 12 Canchis stoppage and roadblocks, rows 23–24. Contemporary Correo and El Búho accounts describe action in five districts and demands concerning interurban fares, budget, routes, local hiring, train service, and constitutional change, but give no crowd count. Planned duration differs across reports and actual physical end is unknown; pending threshold. No participant counts are combined across sites.
- E009: Jan 28 Espinar public assembly beginning an indefinite strike, row 114. Red Muqui reports the assembly underway in the main square, confirming a public physical onset; no attendance estimate establishes 50. Attendance and threshold remain pending. The July 2020 conflict was reported deactivated; Jan 21/25 dialogue steps precede the Jan 28 assembly and are not coded as its on-site response.
- E010: San Miguel/Juliaca gas-price and urban-fare gathering. Correo says more than 20 people gathered on Jan 20, while Report 203 row 84 dates a similar San Miguel action to Jan 21; exact day and row linkage remain unresolved. “More than 20” supports a minimum of 21, not 50, so date precision is month and threshold is pending.
- E011: separate Juliaca march over gas and other prices, described by Correo as the second of two San Román protests. The Jan 21 update and “yesterday” point to Jan 20, but an internal reference to Jan 22 as “today” conflicts; month precision is retained. No attendance count is provided. Its distinction from E010 is provisional; no count is transferred. Threshold remains pending.

Report 203 row disposition and episode boundaries
The 135 numbered actions are accounted for in the first-pass ledger in G009. Sixteen table rows map to nine event candidates: rows 16, 23–24, 31–34, 84, 110, 114, 117, 119–122, and 128 (E001 and E003–E010). Row 84's match to E010 is tentative because the date conflicts. E002 and E011 arise from separate reporting and are not Report 203 row matches. Fifteen rows are recorded as exclusions in excluded.json, each with an explicit private-only or internal/intercommunal rationale; exclusion means outside the current public-state-demand candidate set, not that the action did not occur. The remaining 104 rows are explicit unresolved leads, not eligible-event counts. Their table entries were screened but not independently verified for attendance of at least 50, exact public/state target, physical ending, or relationship to repeated measures.

The ledger preserves unresolved action-unit questions: Report 203 rows 6–7, 17–18, and 97–98 repeat actors or demands across measures; rows 52–53 mention a dialogue table without resolving its public target; school-tuition rows 69 and 113 do not identify ownership or addressee and remain unresolved; row 95 Prosegur names national anti-labor policy and remains a state-policy lead; UTEA rows 81/105 have unclear target and public/private setting; and rows 130–132 and 135 need measure and physical-boundary review. Later FMP dates (rows 47, 57–59, 77, 103, 111) are not automatically merged with Jan 13. See G007 and G009 for the full identified relationships and row ranges.

The November-onset agrarian strike is recorded separately as earlier context in C001. Sources place its onset in Ica on Nov 30 and its spread to La Libertad on Dec 3; a Jan 1 agreement to unblock a highway does not establish a new January onset, continued physical presence, or exact departure. A Jan 4 call is not treated as an executed gathering without physical evidence. December deaths and responses are not transferred. February Report 204 lists later Cusco/Puno actions, but a gap of weeks does not establish continuous physical presence or January event endings (G013).

Unresolved coding and next work
All eleven candidates have unknown physical end dates. All seven state-response dimensions remain unknown across the candidates except that E001's route obstruction is documented separately and does not establish crowd dispersal. E001's protester violence is yes; protester violence for the other candidates is unknown. E004 and E007 alone currently meet the proposed attendance threshold on opened evidence. E010 has a reported minimum of 21; the 50-person threshold remains unverified. The other pending candidates lack an established lower bound of 50. These are candidate-level research assessments, not coordinator decisions.

Thirteen gaps remain in gaps.json, covering baseline and method limits, local-language and regional coverage, attendance, action-unit boundaries, physical endings and responses, Q005 capture loss, retrieval failures, and December/February ownership. Prioritize the 104 unresolved table rows with explicit government or public-policy demands, obtain attendance and physical-boundary evidence, and resolve repeated-measure relationships before proposing more events or exclusions. No temporary servers, browsers, containers, or background processes were started by this unit.
