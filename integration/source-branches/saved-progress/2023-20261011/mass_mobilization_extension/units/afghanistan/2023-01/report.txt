Afghanistan — January 2023 (ccode 700)

Status: open-web research dossier prepared by the assigned worker; candidates and exclusions are provisional pending coordinator review. No candidate has been approved as an official Mass Mobilization observation. The official baseline, coverage universe, schema and reuse terms remain unverified.

Scope and method

The assigned unit is events beginning 1–31 January 2023. Searches also checked December 2022 and February 2023 only for continuity. Discovery covered English, Dari/Persian and Pashto queries, all four January week windows, broad protest terms, and targeted Quran-burning, pension/worker, women/education and adjacent-month searches. The mirror contains 28 unique query records and 651 returned result headers. These are search headers, not 651 articles or independent confirmations.

The dossier contains 11 source cards: 8 marked opened, 2 search-only and 1 blocked after only a subscription-limited opening. Eleven fresh manual-review access receipts are retained. Open web retrieval cannot reproduce the original LexisNexis source set. Dari/Persian reports were summarized in English, but independent human translation review and local-language completeness are not established; no Pashto body was opened.

Candidates and screened leads

• Kabul retirees, 2 January (E001): AAN dates a pensioners’ demonstration; TOLOnews search preview says “dozens” and describes unpaid pensions. TOLOnews itself could not be retrieved after two cache misses and an exact-URL DNS failure. These are technical failures, not denials. AAN cites TOLOnews and is not independent confirmation of the count. “Dozens” does not establish whether 50 or more attended. The reported call on the Islamic Emirate to pay pensions appears only in the TOLOnews search preview; the opened AAN passage establishes a gathering but not its demand or state target. The candidate stays pending_evidence. The Ministry statement about an allocation awaiting approval is not established as a direct response.

• Badakhshan, 27 January (E002): Euronews republishes a Reuters report. A provincial-government spokesperson reports thousands after Friday prayers and gives both foreign-target slogans (“Death to Sweden, U.S.A and U.N”) and a request for the Islamic Emirate to take a strong position. Preserve the mixed targets and pending_state_target status for coordinator interpretation. Reuters was identified as the article’s origin only after the body was opened; Euronews is not independent corroboration. Afghanistan International’s province list is attributed to Bakhtar and does not independently confirm the local count.

• Herat Cinema Square, 27 January (E003): Etilaatroz says protesters called for punishment of those responsible for Quran burning, but does not identify the addressee. It quotes slogans against Sweden and “enemies of Islam.” “Hundreds” is the aggregate for Balkh, Herat, Ghor and Badghis; no share can be assigned to this site. Keep both the target and 50-person threshold unresolved; do not merge this gathering with Badakhshan.

• Kandahar, 26 January (E004): CIR/Afghan Witness reports “several hundred.” This clears the size threshold as reported, but the local demand toward Afghanistan’s government is not established. Keep pending_state_target. The Foreign Ministry condemnation quoted in the report is not linked as a response to this gathering.

Screened separately: Khost on 24 January is excluded as foreign_target_only because AFP reports hundreds chanting against the Swedish government and politicians, with no Afghan-state demand. Mazar-e-Sharif on 27 January is retained as a provisional candidate (E005): Etilaatroz says “dozens,” which has no verified numerical upper bound; threshold and Mazar-specific demand/addressee remain unresolved.

CIR/Afghan Witness also verifies Quran-burning protests in Qalat (Zabul) on 24 January and Tirin Kut (Uruzgan), Asadabad (Kunar) and Sharan (Paktika) on 26 January; it describes protests in most provinces on 27 January, verified footage in 25 of 34 provinces and claims in seven more by 30 January. Except for Kandahar, the report gives no local counts. Etilaatroz reports its four-province aggregate without site-level counts. These leads remain explicit follow-up gaps rather than being merged into one national campaign: the report does not establish centralized national organization, while Etilaatroz attributes coordination to the Taliban.

Unknowns and continuity

No candidate-specific physical end or direct state response is established, so the seven response fields and protester violence remain unknown. The Foreign Ministry’s condemnation of Quran burning, reported as occurring earlier that week/on 29 January, is not treated as a response to a specific protest. Announcements and related official statements are not coded as execution or accommodation without a documented link.

December 2022 searches found university-ban demonstrations, but no January public assembly continuing that activity was verified. February searches found no new gathering establishing continuation of the January Quran or pension episodes; reports of arrests alone are not a protest event. continuations.json therefore remains empty.

Known retrieval limits: the TOLOnews result could not be opened for technical reasons; the Pajhwok report was subscription-limited after the opening; and the Afghan Witness event map rendered only a JavaScript shell. These limits do not establish that no further events occurred.
