Oman, January 2021 - open-web research

Status: researched_open_web_pending_review. This is a provisional research dossier for Oman (provisional ccode 698), with the target event-onset window 1-31 January 2021. The project cutoff is 2026-10-07 inclusive, America/Mexico_City. The current official dataset baseline, country universe, schema, cutoff and supplemental Coding Procedure remain pending coordinator verification.

Outcome: no event candidate beginning in January was identified in the searches and source material accessible in this pass. events.json and continuations.json are empty. This is a scoped discovery result, not a claim that no protest occurred or that local media coverage was exhaustive. No January episode was found to continue from December 2020 or into February 2021. The 11 April 2021 worker sit-in outside the Royal Palace described by GCHR (S026) concerns a separate April action; February is mentioned only as the employer's return to normal operations. Its relationship to the later May unemployment protests was not established, and it was not moved into the January unit.

Search method: 20 individually logged English and Arabic web searches used Oman/January terms, five weekly January windows, local and official outlet searches, sector terms, Arabic protest/strike/sit-in variants, Muscat/Sohar/Salalah regional terms, and December/February boundary checks. Discovery reached Oman Observer, Times of Oman, Muscat Daily, Al Shabiba, Al Roya, Al Watan, Oman Daily archive results and Oman News Agency, alongside international press and rights-organization reporting. Search date limits were unreliable and generic Arabic عمان also returned Jordan results. Q019 and Q020 tool outputs were truncated; visible returned URLs were retained and the capture limitation recorded. Open-web discovery does not reproduce the original LexisNexis method.

Source review: 47 unique source URLs are recorded, 10 opened and 37 search-only. Opened material included the 28 January Ministry of Interior COVID-era suspension of public events (S013) and a same-day English account (S027); these concern public-health measures, not protest or response to a protest. January local reporting described labor complaints and employer/Ministry processes: Oman Observer GFoW reports (S029), an employment assurance (S031), job-nationalization decisions (S033), Al Roya's 17-28 January complaint report (S044), an Oman Daily employment-policy opinion (S046), and an Al Watan committee account of retained workers (S048). The opened Times of Oman report (S049) describes GFoW complaints from 31 January-4 February. These records concern complaints, settlements, meetings, promises or workforce totals, not public attendance or a gathering. Related GFoW articles quote the same federation information stream (G005) and are not counted as independent protest accounts.

Later-period false leads were kept outside January: accessible searches repeatedly returned the May 2021 Sohar and other-city unemployment protests. A detailed GCHR account places a distinct workers' gathering on 11-15 April 2021 (S026). These later actions are not evidence of January activity, and no automatic campaign merge is proposed. The monthly unit is based on event onset, not publication date.

Exclusions: 11 non-event leads are preserved in excluded.json. They include federation labor-complaint figures, Ministry/employer employment negotiations, job-nationalization decisions, vague calls about reopening Friday prayers without an action, an embassy advisory about Washington, and a conditional opinion about possible future accountability demands. Search-result-only worker figures are explicitly not treated as participant counts. The accessible reports do not describe a public gathering, strike or protest in the January unit.

Continuity and response coding: the adjacent-month searches found no documented December-start episode carried into January and no February continuation of a January action. No event exists in this dossier to which attendance, violence or any of the seven state-response dimensions can be coded. Silence in news reports, police monitoring, legal rules and COVID restrictions are not used to infer protest absence or dispersal.

Open gaps: (1) official baseline and additional Coding Procedure pending; (2) open-web search cannot reproduce LexisNexis; (3) Arabic and local archives have not been reviewed comprehensively by a fluent researcher; (4) Times of Oman and Al Shabiba pages had cache misses, and ordinary HTTPS requests to two exact pages failed DNS before an HTTP response; no site denial was observed; (5) several GFoW items derive from the same underlying federation statements; (6) an Oman Daily 4 January archive PDF open timed out before document content was returned; (7) Q019/Q020 web-search outputs were truncated. Details and follow-up are recorded in gaps.json. These limits prevent an exhaustive absence finding.

Counts at close: 20 searches; 47 unique sources (10 opened, 37 search-only); 0 event candidates; 11 exclusions; 0 continuations; 7 gaps. Research status is open-web pending review; no official IDs or approval have been assigned.
