Canada, February 2021 — first-pass research dossier

Scope and method
This first pass records 37 actual open-web search invocations and 1,239 ordered result headers. Searches covered Canada-wide and weekly February windows, city-level queries for major urban centres, English and French terms, and topic leads involving public-health restrictions, labour, Indigenous land and pipeline action, climate, and Indian-farmer solidarity. The fresh extraction was compared with the durable raw captures, mapped query captures, and search_log.jsonl. All 37 invocations and all 1,239 header arrays matched in order, including result order, refs, titles, URLs, tool, and request clocks; there were zero mismatches. Q001’s descriptive wrapper changed from “Original batched invocation” to “One original invocation” in the mapped query label, while its exact four structured search terms are preserved. The original raw extraction manifest and captures remain available under captures/search/capture-history/ and captures/extracted/.

Coverage is open-web discovery, not an exhaustive archive search. It does not establish that every Canadian protest in February was found. LexisNexis, systematic newspaper archives, and a completed official-baseline comparison were not available in this first pass. The official baseline gate remains pending. Search-result snippets served as leads, not event-body evidence. A search for a CBC Braided Warriors report returned zero headers after the search tool reported a robots.txt block; no CBC story page was requested.

The dossier has 31 source cards: 28 marked opened, two search-only, and one blocked. 27 current-unit body opens and 1 prior-unit-only body consultation are recorded; S005 is the saved January review. S005’s Calgary turnout information is carried from the saved January-unit body review; no February body request was made for that URL. Each opened card records its actual written-body scope and source lineage. No full article or PDF body was retained. Embedded videos were not played, and image pixels were not inspected. The selected scope and all retrieval attempts are in sources.jsonl.

Provisional event candidates
Seventeen candidates are recorded. Five have provisional proposed_include eligibility, subject to coordinator review and the baseline gate:
- CA202102-E001: Edmonton’s Feb. 20 Walk for Freedom rally. Contemporary reports say hundreds gathered at the legislature. Only the Edmonton assembly has qualifying crowd evidence; no attendance is assigned to route stops in Lethbridge, Calgary, or Red Deer. The organizer relationship between Walk for Freedom Alberta and Freedom Unity Alliance remains unresolved, and police-reported assaults are not attributed to either side.
- CA202102-E003: Feb. 13 Kelowna rally at Stuart Park. Global News reports more than two hundred at opening. Whether the recurring gatherings over preceding months were discrete rallies or a continuous episode remains open.
- CA202102-E008: Feb. 6 Montreal protest against police-based pandemic policy and curfew. The Link reports about 100 at the initial gathering, growing to almost 250 before the march; Journal Métro independently adds route and speech context. Police presence is not coded as dispersal, and physical end and state response remain unknown.
- CA202102-E012: Feb. 28 Toronto protest outside Mayor John Tory’s residence. CIVICUS reports about 800; contemporary Spring reporting corroborates the One Bedford Road site, organizer and demand to stop city injunctions and police harassment of encampments, but not the turnout. The preceding injunction is not treated as the response to this event; physical end and response remain unknown.
- CA202102-E016: Feb. 21 Magog march, about 80 according to the local French report. The account gives a route to Parc des Braves and reports five mask tickets and one cellphone-driving citation; tickets are not arrests. Start time, organizer, precise policy demands, and physical ending remain unresolved.

Eight additional candidates have pending_threshold status: the Edmonton counter-protest (E002), the Vancouver Feb. 27 Extinction Rebellion action (E006), Vancouver’s Feb. 20 Art Gallery rally (E007), the Montreal Feb. 7 ministry-office gathering (E009), Belleville city workers (E010), Victoria’s Indian-farmer vehicle rally (E011), the Feb. 19 Braided Warriors sit-in (E013), and Calgary’s Chinook Centre rally (E015). Descriptions such as “dozens” or “small group,” a headcount of police, represented union membership, combined opposing-side turnout, and a vehicle count do not establish 50 protesters. E006’s approximately 50 is retained as a threshold uncertainty. E011’s more-than-100 vehicles are not a person count, and its principal demand concerns the Indian government.

CA202102-E005 is pending_unit: the Calgary Feb. 27 account gives approximately 400 protesters and counter-protesters together without a side-specific count or clear group boundaries. CA202102-E004 is pending_evidence because the separate Kelowna counter-protest is described only as small/few and a state-directed demand is not established. CA202102-E014 is a separate late-February Braided Warriors report with only month precision; its attendance, exact site, and demand are absent, so it is not merged with the Feb. 19 sit-in. CA202102-E017 concerns GraceLife Church’s regular Feb. 14 worship service during a closure order. AHS counted about 332 in the sanctuary and about 57 listening from the lobby; an applicants’ later filing says the sermon criticized provincial COVID policy and the church’s noncompliance. Whether the congregation assembled to make a demand of government remains unresolved, so the service is pending_evidence. Pastor James Coates’s Feb. 16 arrest is not moved to the Sunday gathering.

Exclusions, continuing episodes, and open gaps
Four leads remain explicitly excluded: X001 is a Peterborough rally on March 13, outside the month; X002 is a Feb. 7 Hamilton rally with four reported attendees, below 50; X003 is a separate Jan. 17 Hamilton rally, outside the month; and X004 is Ottawa’s daily Indian-farmer solidarity demonstration at the Indian High Commission, whose target is another country’s policy. The codebook’s foreign-state-target rule is applied. A planned Vancouver Feb. 20 rally remains an occurrence gap because the saved lead does not show that it happened. continuations.json is empty; that records no established out-of-month continuation in this pass, not complete adjacent-month coverage.

The 28 detailed gaps retain unresolved event boundaries, attendance, organizer identity, demand, state target, physical end, response, continuity, and source lineage. Examples include the Edmonton convoy route versus attendance at its sole documented assembly; combined turnout for Calgary’s opposing sides; possible recurring Kelowna gatherings; threshold evidence for counter-protests and labour actions; and the distinct Feb. 19 and late-February Braided Warriors reports. State-response values not explicitly reported remain unknown. Protester violence is not inferred from police-reported injuries or arrests unless the account attributes conduct.

Access and retrieval history
Forty-two receipt files and their hashes are inventoried in access_receipts/manifest.json. Every body request is represented with the corresponding source’s publisher/origin review, receipt, attempt history, and read scope. A receipt marked manual_review is not retrieval authorization. R024 records an existing exact-URL hold for Hamilton Police S016; no body request was made. R028 is retained as a defer with an unstable input fingerprint and no body request. R007 revalidates previously saved January evidence for S005 without reacquiring its body.

Cache miss and DNS errors are not treated as denials. For Edmonton CityNews S002, the initial wrapper cache miss and one DNS failure were followed by a fresh receipt and ordinary public HTTPS 200. VPD S007’s first 200 response yielded navigation only; a fresh R011 preceded the identical response whose corrected parser surfaced the release body. The Montreal Global News page S013 had the same navigation-only parser issue; fresh R021 preceded a second identical response that exposed its VideoObject description. The Link S011 and Journal Métro S012 cache misses were followed by fresh receipts before ordinary HTTPS responses. Daily Hive S024 remained search-only after a cache miss and DNS failure. Global News video S029 remained search-only after a cache miss; no fallback was attempted. No credentials, cookies, proxies, custom headers, or access bypasses were used, and no full bodies were retained.
